Driving in Georgia: your licence, the border car insurance law, road dangers and what to do after a crash

Driving in Georgia is allowed on a UK licence for up to a year, but country roads, nights, animals on the road and mountain winters call for care. This guide covers your licence and permit, the law on compulsory cover for cars registered abroad, hiring a car, road and winter hazards, alcohol, and what to do after a crash. Facts come from the law on matsne and from UK and Canadian official travel advice. Sources are at the end.

Your licence and the international driving permit

A UK licence is enough for up to a year, though a hire company may want a permit as well. The UK safety and security advice says: "You can drive in Georgia with a UK driving licence for up to one year."

The 1968 permit. If you are hiring a car, GOV.UK says to check with the company before you travel "whether they will accept the UK licence or whether you will need a 1968 international driving permit (IDP)". It adds: "You cannot buy an IDP outside the UK, so get one before you travel."

Other licences. The Government of Canada's travel advice to its own travellers, updated 27 August 2026, tells holders of a Canadian licence that "it is recommended that you obtain a notarized translation of your driver's licence into Georgian". It also recommends an International Driving Permit, "as some car rental agencies or insurance providers may require it".

Our tip. Licence from another country? Read your own government's advice. Keep the licence, IDP and any notarized translation with your passport whenever you are at the wheel.

Georgia car insurance at the border: the law

If you arrive in a car registered abroad, you must buy cover for the harm it may cause to other people, and keep it for your whole stay. The rule is set by Law No 1775 of 15 December 2017 on compulsory civil liability cover for owners of foreign-registered vehicles, published in English on matsne.

Who must buy it. Article 3(1) says that when a vehicle "registered in a foreign country enters the territory", its "owner/driver… is obliged to insure his/her civil liability… during the full period of his/her stay". The policy may not be shorter than the periods in Article 5, and Article 3 has applied "from 1 March 2018".

What it costs by law. Article 5 fixes the premium by vehicle type and period. Here are the two rows most visitors need, as set in the law's text:

GEL by vehicle (Article 5)15 days30 days90 days1 year
Car, max. authorised mass up to 3,500 kg, up to eight passenger seats besides the driver305090295
Motorcycle with an engine over 50 cm³203570215

The shortest term in the table is 15 days. We read these amounts on matsne on 19 November 2026; check the current price when you buy the policy.

How much it pays out. Article 6 sets a limit of GEL 30,000 for harm to the life and health of each injured person and GEL 300,000 for each event. Article 6(2) caps an injured person's medical treatment at half of that GEL 30,000; the full sum is for disability or death. Article 7 caps property damage at GEL 25,000 for each injured party and GEL 50,000 for each event.

What it does not do. In our reading of Articles 6–7, this is third-party cover: it pays people you injure and owners of things you damage, up to those limits. It does not pay for your own doctor or hospital. For that, visitors need health and accident cover of at least 30,000 GEL for the whole stay; see the entry rule on travel insurance.

Where to buy it. Under Article 3, the patrol police or customs at the border explain the duty to buy the policy "at the border checkpoint"; it can be bought from an insurer, agent or broker, or "by means of electric payment", and it takes effect from the moment of payment. Our tip: do not drive on before you have paid, and keep the policy in the car until you leave.

Renting a car in Georgia

Before you sign, check your papers, the cover and the car itself. The border law applies to vehicles registered abroad. Our tip: a car you hire in the country is usually registered there, so ask the company what cover it includes.

Papers. Ask the company before the trip whether your licence is enough or an IDP is needed; some rental agencies may require one.

Picking up on arrival. For taxis, money and the first hours after landing, read our Tbilisi airport guide.

Our tip: before you drive off.

  • Ask in writing what the rental cover pays for harm to other people, for damage to the car itself, and what you pay yourself after a crash.
  • Photograph every scratch with the agent. GOV.UK warns that "many cars are poorly maintained".
  • Check the tyres, lights, seatbelts and spare wheel.
  • Going to the mountains in winter? Ask for snow tyres or chains: you must have them there.

Georgia roads: what to expect

Main roads in the cities are usually fine; the countryside is harder. Roads are generally in good condition in Tbilisi and in major cities, but in rural areas they can be poorly maintained and dangerous because of:

  • unpaved and uneven surfaces;
  • raised and sunken manholes;
  • stray livestock;
  • lack of traffic signs and insufficient lighting;
  • slippery roads and winter conditions, especially in the mountains.

In rural areas, traffic signs may be in Georgian only.

What GOV.UK adds. Its list of conditions says that "road markings and the right of way can be confusing", "many cars are poorly maintained" and "driving can be erratic". Outside Tbilisi, especially in remote areas, it advises making sure your vehicle "is suitable for off-road driving" and checking the weather locally.

Priority to the right. Georgia uses the "priority to the right" system. At intersections you must give way to vehicles coming from the right if you are not in a priority lane, and to vehicles entering roundabouts.

Other road users. Fatal accidents are frequent and among the main causes of death in Georgia. It is common for people at the wheel to change lanes often, speed, drink and engage in road rage, and that pedestrians often cross in the middle of the road. The advice for drivers: always drive defensively, ignore road rage, plan your trip and carry a phone and charger. For crime and other risks, read Is Georgia safe?

Closures. The Roads Department publishes a road restrictions page on georoad.gov.ge. The mountain road north to Stepantsminda is covered in our Kazbegi guide.

Night driving, mountains and winter

Plan to arrive before dark, and in the cold months expect snow and closures high up. GOV.UK says: "Take care when driving, especially at night," and warns that "roads outside city centres are often badly lit and poor quality". Other official advice is shorter: avoid travelling after dark.

Rain, snow and landslides. GOV.UK says that "heavy rain, flooding and snow at higher altitudes can affect roads and bridges making travel difficult or impossible (particularly in remote areas)". It adds that "landslides are common".

Winter tyres. If you are driving in mountainous areas in winter, you must have snow tires or chains on your vehicle.

Our tip: on a mountain day.

  • Set off early so the hardest part is behind you before dusk.
  • Check georoad.gov.ge restrictions before a pass.
  • Fill the tank, and carry water, warm clothes and a charged phone.
  • If it is snowing hard or the road is closed, wait.

Drink driving

If you are at the wheel, do not drink at all. GOV.UK says: "Drink driving punishments can be severe. Georgia has a lower blood alcohol content limit than the UK, so avoid alcohol if driving."

We give no figure for the limit: our sources do not quote one. Official advice also lists driving under the influence among common local habits, so, our tip, take extra care late in the evening.

Our tip. If wine is part of the evening, leave the car at the hotel. Regulated taxis are white with roof-mounted taxi signs; use only officially marked ones.

After a crash: 112, medical care and insurance

Make the scene safe, dial 112, then tell your insurer. The UK health advice says: "Dial 112 and ask for an ambulance." Here is how to call an ambulance on 112 and what to say.

Help may take time. Police response times in remote areas and outside larger cities can be significantly longer. GOV.UK adds: "Outside Tbilisi, medical facilities are limited."

Your policy. The same GOV.UK page says: "Contact your insurance company quickly if you're referred to a medical facility for treatment." In our reading, your own care is a matter for your health cover, and harm to others goes to the third-party policy or the rental cover.

Stay at the scene. For a car registered abroad, Article 8(1) of Law No 1775 says the owner/driver must report the crash at once to 112 and "remain at the scene of the traffic accident and wait for the arrival of an authorised person", unless someone urgently needs to get to a medical facility.

Our tip: what to do, step by step.

  1. Switch on the hazard lights and move everyone away from traffic if it is safe.
  2. Dial 112. Give the road name, the nearest village or a map pin.
  3. Do not move someone who is badly hurt unless they are in danger where they lie.
  4. Photograph the scene and number plates; note everyone's names and phone numbers.
  5. Call the hire company and the assistance line on your health policy.
  6. Keep every paper and treatment receipt.

Doctors for injuries. The catalogue of orthopaedic and trauma doctors in Tbilisi on 100doc.ge lists specialists who treat injuries.

Not at the wheel? The Government of Canada's travel advice says marshrutka minibuses "are poorly maintained and often lack security features like seatbelts" and advises avoiding them.

Frequently asked questions

What do I need to drive in Georgia?

A valid licence. GOV.UK says a UK licence works for up to one year; a hire company may also ask for a 1968 IDP. The Government of Canada recommends that its travellers carry a notarized translation and an IDP.

Do I need car insurance at the border?

Yes, if your car is registered abroad. Law No 1775 requires third-party cover for your whole stay; by the law's text, 15 days cost GEL 30 for a car. Check the current price when you buy.

Does the border policy pay for my own injuries?

No, in our reading of Articles 6–7: it covers harm you cause to others, with treatment of an injured person capped at half of the GEL 30,000 limit. For your own care you need health and accident cover of at least 30,000 GEL.

Can I have one drink and still drive?

Better not. GOV.UK says the blood alcohol limit is lower than in the UK and punishments can be severe, so avoid alcohol completely.

Do I need winter tyres?

In the mountains in winter, yes: you must have snow tires or chains. Check road restrictions on georoad.gov.ge before a pass.

Is it safe to drive at night?

It is riskier. GOV.UK warns that roads outside city centres are often badly lit and that stray livestock can be a hazard; other official advice is to avoid travel after dark.

What should I do after an accident?

Make the scene safe, dial 112 and ask for an ambulance if anyone is hurt, then call your health cover's assistance line and the hire company.
