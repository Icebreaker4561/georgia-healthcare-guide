Taxi in Tbilisi and Georgia: apps, the airport fare, official taxis, trips between cities and staying safe

A taxi in Tbilisi is easiest to get through an app: the UK government advises pre-booking rides that way rather than flagging cars down in the street. This guide covers which apps work in Georgia, the fixed fare from Tbilisi Airport and taxis at Kutaisi and Batumi. It also explains how to spot an official taxi, how to pay, trips between cities and what to do if something goes wrong. Facts come from the airports' and the ride services' own sites, the national emergency line and UK and Canadian government advice. Sources are at the end.

Taxi apps in Georgia: Bolt, Yandex Go and what about Uber

No need to hail a car: to get a taxi in Georgia, use a ride app. Bolt lists ten Georgian cities, and Yandex Go has a page for the country. The Government of Canada notes that "Ride-sharing services are also popular throughout Georgia."

Bolt in Georgia: which cities

Bolt in Georgia covers ten cities on its list of cities: Bakuriani, Batumi, Gori, Gudauri/Stepantsminda, Kutaisi, Poti, Rustavi, Tbilisi, Telavi and Zugdidi.

In the capital. Bolt's city page promises a ride "within minutes". Its categories include Taxi Tbilisi, described as "Ride in bus lane", and Economy.

Flying in. The service's list of airports includes Tbilisi, Kutaisi and Batumi.

Paying. Bolt's pages we checked don't say which payment methods work here.

Our tip. Before you confirm the ride, check the category, the price and the payment method on the screen.

Yandex Go in Georgia: what its page says

Yandex Go's English page for the country says you can "request rides and order item delivery in Georgia". It adds: "Pay with cash or by card."

The page names no cities, so check whether the service covers the place you are in before you rely on it.

Is there Uber in Georgia? And Maxim?

Georgia is not on Uber's list of countries, so plan on another service. Maxim's country page does not list it either; we could not confirm that the service works here.

How to take a Tbilisi taxi safely, step by step

Pre-book a ride and never stop a car in the street. The UK government's travel advice says "Taxi provision and safety has improved significantly" and lists three rules: "do not flag taxis in the street", "try to pre-book taxis through an app" and check for "working seatbelts".

Whatever taxi service in Tbilisi you choose, the same steps apply:

  1. Book. Use an app. At a hotel, the Government of Canada's advice is to "ask for your taxi at the front desk if you’re using hotel services".
  2. Our tip. Before you get in, make sure the car and the plate match the booking.
  3. Price. Agree it first: "negotiate the fare in advance if there is no meter".
  4. Belt. Check that the belt works before the car moves off.
  5. Pay. Keep change ready: "have small bills available for payment".

More on the capital in Is Tbilisi Safe Day and Night?

How to recognise an official taxi in Tbilisi

The Government of Canada's travel advice gives two signs of an official taxi: in Georgia, taxis "are regulated and are recognizable by their white colour and roof-mounted taxi signs". It asks travellers to "use only officially marked taxis".

Unofficial cars. The same advice warns: "Unofficial taxis continue to operate, and drivers generally don’t use meters. They may overcharge you for the ride."

Our tip. If a car has no roof sign and you didn't book it, order a ride instead.

Airport taxis: Tbilisi, Kutaisi and Batumi

All three international airports list taxi services at the terminal, but only the capital's publishes a fixed fare.

Tbilisi Airport taxi: the fixed fare

The fixed rate is 40 GEL to the city centre and 50 GEL to suburban areas, according to the airport's site, checked on 22 November 2026. Cars wait "outside the terminal, just at the curbside of the Arrival Hall" with a "24-hour service", and the ride takes "20-30 minutes, depending on traffic". The page gives no date for the fares.

Booking online. Bolt's page for the capital offers to "Request a ride to and from Tbilisi airports".

Booking ahead. The Government of Canada's advice is to "arrange your pickup with a reputable company before arrival when travelling by air, or use authorized cabs at the airport".

The bus. Bus 337 costs 1 GEL; times and stops are in Tbilisi Airport to the City.

Our tip. Ignore offers of a ride inside the arrivals hall: walk out to the kerb or book a ride.

Kutaisi: the airport taxi and Bolt

Kutaisi International Airport's site lists one taxi operator, working 24/7 in the arrivals hall, and three bus transfer operators, also 24/7. It gives no prices.

Distance and rides. The airport lies 14 km west of the city, according to Bolt. Its Kutaisi page lists XL Travel, "Large vehicles with seating for 6", and Bolt Travel, "comfortable intercity rides". Economy is not listed there, so check the app for rides in town. More in Arriving at Kutaisi Airport.

Batumi: taxis at the terminal exit

In the words of Batumi airport's site: "Taxis are available right upon the exit of the terminal; it is approximately 10-15 minute drive to the city centre." Municipal bus No10 runs on a fixed route, Airport–Batumi Centre.

Ride categories. Bolt's Batumi page lists XL Travel, with seating for 6, and Economy.

Taxi prices in Tbilisi and how to pay

The only official price we found is the fixed airport rate above. Ride services don't publish prices on the pages we checked, so check the price in the app before you confirm.

Paying. Payment methods for the apps are covered above. With a driver you hire directly, keep small notes ready.

Lari and cards. For money in general, see Cash or Card in Georgia?

Our tip. Settle the amount in lari, not dollars or euros, and say the address before the car moves off.

Taxi between cities: Kutaisi, Batumi, Kazbegi and Yerevan

We found no official fare for trips between cities, so agree it before you set off. Bolt's Kutaisi page lists Bolt Travel, "comfortable intercity rides".

Roads. In the Government of Canada's words, "Roads are generally in good condition in Tbilisi and in major cities, but in rural areas, they are sometimes poorly maintained" and can be dangerous. More in Roads, Taxis and Marshrutkas: the Bigger Risk.

Our tip. Avoid long drives after dark on mountain roads, and plan the way back before you leave. If the driver goes too fast, ask them to slow down or stop.

Other ways. Trains and minibuses are alternatives on some of these routes: Tbilisi to Batumi Train and Back, Tbilisi to Kazbegi: the Georgian Military Road, Tbilisi to Borjomi: Getting There and Marshrutka in Georgia. In the capital, the Tbilisi Metro is the alternative to a car.

Tbilisi to Yerevan by taxi

Neither the UK nor the Canadian advice we checked describes the land border with Armenia or a ride to Yerevan, so we give no route, time or price. The UK government's advice for Armenia mentions "the cross-border train service between Armenia and Georgia". See Is There a Tbilisi to Yerevan Train?

Safety in the car: seatbelts, the back seat and night rides

The UK government's advice for the car itself is to "check the taxi has working seatbelts".

Our tip. Wear the belt in the back seat too.

Seatbelts and travelling with children

We found no official rule on child seats in hired cars that we could check.

Our tip. If you travel with a small child, ask the service about a child seat before you book, or bring your own.

The back seat and travelling alone

The Government of Canada's advice is to "Use reputable taxi services or ride-sharing services and sit in the back seat". It also asks travellers to "never share a taxi with strangers". More in Is Georgia Safe for Solo Female Travellers?

If you feel threatened. In its safety advice, the UK government says: "If someone attempts to assault you or you feel threatened, call the emergency number 112 and ask for the police."

Taxis at night

The UK government's general advice: "Take usual precautions after dark in unfamiliar areas."

Our tip. At night, book through the app, send your route to a friend and wait for the car indoors.

After a crash or if you feel unsafe: call 112

Dial 112 at once: it is Georgia's "single emergency number", and its call-takers handle calls 24/7 for the police, fire and rescue and the ambulance.

What to say. Start with where it happened, "the exact location of the incident". More in What to Tell the Operator.

In English. The Government of Canada's advice mentions "the 112 Georgia mobile application to contact emergency service providers in English". No SIM card, or can't talk? See No SIM Card, No Credit on the Phone or Can't Talk.

Driving yourself? See What to Do After a Road Accident.

If anyone is hurt, call the emergency number first.

Frequently asked questions

Which taxi app works in Tbilisi?

Bolt lists the capital among its ten cities in the country, and Yandex Go has an English page for Georgia.

Is there Uber in Tbilisi?

Not as far as we can tell: Uber's list of countries does not include Georgia, and Maxim's country page does not list it either.

What cities does Bolt cover in Georgia?

Bakuriani, Batumi, Gori, Gudauri/Stepantsminda, Kutaisi, Poti, Rustavi, Tbilisi, Telavi and Zugdidi.

How much is a taxi from Tbilisi Airport to the city centre?

A fixed 40 GEL to the centre and 50 GEL to suburban areas, as listed on the official taxi page (checked 22 November 2026); the ride takes 20–30 minutes.

Is it cheaper to book a car in advance or on arrival?

We can't compare: ride services don't publish prices on the pages we checked, while the taxi rate at Tbilisi Airport is fixed. Official advice lists both options: pre-book with a reputable company or use authorized cabs on arrival.

How much is a taxi from Kutaisi to Tbilisi?

We found no official price for this trip. Whichever way you book, agree the fare before you set off.

Is it safe to take a taxi in Georgia?

The UK government says taxi safety "has improved significantly". Its advice: don't flag cars in the street, pre-book a ride and check the seatbelts work.

What number do I call after a taxi accident?

112, the single emergency number, answered 24/7; its page lists the police, fire and rescue and the ambulance.
