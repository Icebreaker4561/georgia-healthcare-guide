> [!facts] In short
> - Wash the wound with soap and running water for 15 minutes.[^1][^4]
> - See a doctor the same day; do not wait to watch the animal.[^4]
> - The doctor decides on the course, usually three or four visits.[^2]

This page is about the country in the Caucasus, not the US state, and is part of the [Georgia Travel Guide](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/). Checked on 17 November 2026.

## What to do after a dog bite or scratch in Georgia {#first-aid}

<!-- схема: img/rabies-first-steps.webp | Infographic: five first steps after an animal bite or scratch — wash the wound for 15 minutes, see a doctor the same day, start the shots without waiting to watch the animal, finish the course, call your insurer | Wash, see a doctor, start and finish the course; call the assistance line as soon as you can -->

The World Health Organization (WHO) advises you to "wash the wound with water and soap for at least 15 minutes" ([fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/rabies)).[^1]

The country's National Center for Disease Control and Public Health (NCDC) agrees and adds iodine or a similar disinfectant ([NCDC page, in Georgian](https://test.ncdc.ge/Pages/User/LetterContent.aspx?ID=b7abd370-4d70-47f1-8312-4d75e4465f4c&language=en-US)).[^4] Then:

- Saliva in your eyes, nose or mouth? Rinse with water and see a doctor too.[^4]
- Do not wait to see if the animal falls ill: NCDC says treatment must not be delayed to observe it.[^4]
- If the animal has an owner, note their contacts and ask if it is vaccinated.
- Heavy bleeding? Call 112 — here is [how to call an ambulance](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/ambulance-georgia/).

<!-- схема: img/who-rabies-fact-sheet.webp | Excerpt from the WHO rabies fact sheet with the advice to wash the wound with water and soap for at least 15 minutes highlighted | The fact sheet advice: water and soap, at least 15 minutes (screenshot, 17 November 2026) -->

### Do you need a rabies shot after a scratch or a lick? {#exposure-categories}

It depends on the contact; the doctor decides, using three WHO categories:[^1][^2]

- **Category I**: touching, feeding, or a lick on intact skin — no vaccine.
- **Category II**: a nibble on uncovered skin, or minor scratches without bleeding — wash the wound and start the vaccine at once.
- **Category III**: bites or scratches through the skin, saliva on broken skin or on the eyes, nose or mouth, or contact with bats — vaccine plus immunoglobulin.

<!-- схема: img/rabies-exposure-categories.webp | Infographic: WHO categories of exposure — category I no vaccine, category II vaccine, category III vaccine and immunoglobulin | Three categories of exposure and the treatment each one needs -->

### The dog had an ear tag — do you still need to see a doctor? {#ear-tag}

Yes, the same day. We found no official definition of what an ear tag guarantees, from the National Food Agency (NFA) or the city hall. Whether you need a course depends on the contact, not the tag.

## Where to get a rabies vaccine in Tbilisi {#where-tbilisi}

After a dog bite in Tbilisi, go to the Immunization Centre, a private company, at 10a Tashkent Street, phone 032 231 22 78.[^7] According to [its website (in Georgian)](https://imuncentri.ge/index.html), emergency anti-rabies and tetanus immunisation is available every day, around the clock; follow-up doses are best done between 9:00 and 18:00.[^7]

- A surgeon or rabiologist sees you in person and decides whether you need a course, conditional or full.[^7]
- There is no charge for the consultation.[^7]
- Cases are not assessed by phone, so come in.[^7]
- Bring your passport or another ID document.[^7]

We found no official list of clinics in Batumi or other towns. Outside the capital, go to the nearest hospital the same day; for heavy bleeding, dial 112.

## How many rabies shots you need: the WHO schedule {#schedule}

Usually three or four visits over one to four weeks; the doctor chooses the schedule.[^2] For a first course after category II or III exposure, the [WHO position paper of 2018](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/immunization/position_paper_documents/rabies/pp-rabies-summary-2018.pdf?sfvrsn=c9d92ce5_2) lists:[^2]

| Schedule | Days |
|---|---|
| Intradermal (2 sites) | 0, 3, 7[^2] |
| Intramuscular (1 site), Essen | 0, 3, 7, and one dose between 14 and 28[^2] |
| Intramuscular, Zagreb | 0 (2 sites), 7, 21[^2] |
| US CDC, intramuscular | 0, 3, 7, 14[^3] |

The US row is from the [CDC guide to travel medicine](https://www.cdc.gov/yellow-book/hcp/travel-associated-infections-diseases/rabies.html); a fifth dose on day 28 is only for a weakened immune system.[^3] Clinics in Georgia may follow a different schedule; the NCDC page does not list days.[^4]

Had a full course before the trip? You need two doses, on days 0 and 3, and no immunoglobulin; if that course ended less than three months ago, WHO says wound care alone is enough.[^2] Pregnancy and infancy are not a reason to refuse treatment, NCDC says.[^4] Doses go into the upper arm (the thigh under age two), never the buttock.[^2]

<!-- схема: img/rabies-vaccine-clinic.webp | Paper-cut illustration: a nurse gives a vaccine injection in the arm of a traveller with a bandaged hand | Doses go into the upper arm; follow-up visits are best in the daytime -->

### When you also need rabies immunoglobulin {#immunoglobulin}

Only for category III.[^2] It is given once, as early as possible with the first dose and no later than seven days after that first dose, as much as possible into the wound.[^2]

If none is available, the vaccine still starts at once: "Vaccines should never be withheld, regardless of the availability of RIG" ([2018 position paper](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/immunization/position_paper_documents/rabies/pp-rabies-summary-2018.pdf?sfvrsn=c9d92ce5_2)).[^2]

## Who pays for rabies shots in Georgia {#who-pays}

The Immunization Centre gives its price by phone and says emergency immunisation is covered by universal and/or private insurance.[^7] We found no official confirmation that the state pays for rabies shots for visitors.

Since 1 January 2026, foreigners must enter with health and accident cover of 30,000 GEL, as Georgian law requires ([the 2026 entry insurance rule](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/#entry-insurance)).[^8]

A policy covers events during its term, so arrange it before the trip, not after a bite. Still choosing? Here is [travel insurance for visitors to Georgia](https://100doc.ge/tourist/en), an IRAO plan. Its policy wording lists "Emergency Vaccination", including the full anti-rabies course, at 100 %, with no deductible and no separate limit within the 30,000 GEL aggregated limit. Routine vaccination is excluded; over 65, 50 % of costs is reimbursed. The Light plan costs from €0.78 (2.22 GEL, ≈ $0.86) a day on a 360-day policy.

Call the IRAO assistance line, +995 32 2 949 949, as soon as you can, ideally before you go to the clinic: under the policy wording, costs incurred without contacting it may not be reimbursed. Paying yourself at a non-partner clinic? Keep Form IV-100a signed and stamped by the clinic, the itemised bill and original receipts; submit them within 30 calendar days.

## Stray dogs in Tbilisi and across Georgia: how common is rabies? {#stray-dogs}

The disease is present here, mostly in animals. Worldwide, bites and scratches from dogs cause 99 % of human cases ([WHO fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/rabies)).[^1]

By 15 April 2026, three laboratory-confirmed animal cases had been recorded nationwide, the NFA said, as reported by JAMnews. One was a stray dog in Bandza, Martvili, that bit several people; another, a fox in Tsalka. Both areas were put under quarantine.

NCDC reported one human case in 2021 ([NCDC annual report, 2022](https://test.ncdc.ge/Handlers/GetFile.ashx?ID=26d04482-9587-48bb-b746-c9b4dcf01734));[^5] we found no NCDC data for 2022–2026.

## Do tourists need a rabies vaccine before travelling to Georgia? {#before-travel}

It depends on your trip; decide with a doctor before you go.

The UK travel health service [TravelHealthPro](https://travelhealthpro.org.uk/countries/georgia) says rabies "is considered a risk" in the country and has been reported in domestic animals.[^6] It recommends a pre-trip course for people at risk through their work, for trips to areas where treatment is hard to reach, for higher-risk activities such as running or cycling, and for stays longer than a month.[^6] NCDC places the country in a high-risk region.[^4]

The CDC pre-trip schedule is two doses, on days 0 and 7.[^3] It does not replace treatment after exposure.[^3]

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

### Is 7 days too late for a rabies vaccine after a bite?

Do not decide on your own that it is too late: see a doctor today, whatever day it is. WHO and NCDC say treatment should start as soon as possible, and the doctor decides on the course.[^2][^4]

### Can I wait a day after a dog bite?

No. Wash the wound at once and see a doctor the same day: WHO and NCDC say treatment should start immediately.[^1][^4]

### What is the 10-day rule for dog bites?

You may hear about watching the animal for 10 days, but NCDC says observing it must not delay treatment; the doctor decides on the course.[^4]

### Is rabies a problem in Georgia?

In animals, yes: the NFA reported three confirmed cases by April 2026, and NCDC places the country in a high-risk region.[^4] NCDC reported one human case in 2021.[^5]

### Do I need a rabies vaccine for Georgia?

Ask a doctor before the trip: TravelHealthPro recommends one for long stays, remote trips, running or cycling, and work-related risk.[^6] After a bite, yes, if the doctor prescribes it.

### Do tourists pay for rabies shots in Georgia?

We found no official answer. The Immunization Centre quotes its price by phone;[^7] the IRAO travel policy lists the full anti-rabies course.
