## Tbilisi to Telavi: how to get there and how long it takes {#getting-there}

Plan on a little under two hours by road. Georgian National Tourism Administration gives 1 h 55 min on its page about the town[^1] and 1 h 50 min in its article about the mountain road.[^2]

The direct route crosses the range at 1,620 m.[^2] The pass road, which starts from Tbilisi near Vaziani, is 65 km long, the tourism administration says.[^2] We give no total distance from central Tbilisi: we found no official figure, and the unofficial ones we saw do not agree.

There is a second, slower road that avoids the pass. According to the tourism administration, going round the mountains adds about 40 minutes to the trip.[^2] When that is worth it is covered in [the pass or the detour](#gombori-or-detour) below.

<!-- схема: img/telavi-gombori-road.webp | Map-style infographic: the 65 km road that starts from Tbilisi near Vaziani and crosses the Gombori Pass at 1,620 m to Telavi, and the detour via Sagarejo and Gurjaani that adds about 40 minutes | The pass road tops out at 1,620 m; the detour round the mountains adds about 40 minutes -->

You can make the trip three ways:

- **Your own or a hired car.** You choose the road, the time and the stops.
- **Taxi or private driver.** Agree the price and the route before you set off.
- **Shared minibus.** We found no official timetable or fare (see [below](#marshrutka)).

### By car or taxi over the pass {#by-car}

You decide when to leave, which way to go and where to stop. With a taxi, agree the fare and the route before you get in, including whether the driver goes over the pass or round it.

The UK government's travel advice warns that "roads outside city centres are often badly lit and poor quality".[^13] On a mountain road that matters most after dark, in rain and in fog.

If you are driving yourself, read [Driving in Georgia](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/driving-in-georgia/) before the trip. It covers what to expect on Georgian roads in general.

**Our tip.** Leave Tbilisi in the morning and cross the pass in daylight. If you want to stop for views on the way, add time for it, and pull over only where there is room to do so safely.

### By marshrutka (minibus) {#marshrutka}

We found no official information on minibuses between Tbilisi and the town: no timetable, fare or departure station, so we give none.

Unofficial websites name different stations and departure times, so we do not repeat them. Ask your guesthouse or hotel in Tbilisi, and check again on the day you travel.

What to expect on board, how to pay and how to stay safe in a shared van are covered in [Marshrutka in Georgia](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/marshrutka-georgia/).

**Our tip.** If you do take one, ask the driver which way they go: over the pass or round it. If you get carsick, the answer matters, and so does a seat near the front.

### Can you visit Telavi as a day trip from Tbilisi? {#day-trip}

Yes. The tourism administration gives a little under two hours from Tbilisi,[^1] and the return should take about as long, so going there and back in one day is realistic.

The day gets long if you add the sights around the town. Alaverdi Monastery in Kakheti is a 30-minute drive from the town, and Gremi a 15 to 20-minute drive, according to the tourism administration.[^4][^6] Add those drives to the time from Tbilisi and back when you plan the day.

<!-- схема: img/telavi-day-trip.webp | Infographic: drive times from the tourism administration — Tbilisi to Telavi about 1 h 55 min, Telavi to Gremi 15–20 min, Telavi to Alaverdi Monastery about 30 min | The tourism administration gives the time from Tbilisi one way only; allow about as long for the way back -->

In summer, start early and read [the section on heat](#summer-heat) first: the hottest hours fall in the middle of the day, just when you would be walking between sights.

### Telavi to Sighnaghi, Tusheti and back to Tbilisi {#onward}

From the town you can go on to other parts of the region or head for the mountains. For the next stops, see our guides [Sighnaghi and Kakheti](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/sighnaghi/) and [Tusheti](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/tusheti/).

The tourism administration gives the travel time from Tbilisi only;[^1] expect the trip from Telavi to Tbilisi to take about as long. Everything below about the pass, the weather and the detour applies both ways.

## Gombori Pass: the mountain road from Tbilisi to Telavi {#gombori-pass}

It is an asphalt mountain road, open all year, that climbs to 1,620 m above sea level, according to the tourism administration.[^2] It starts from Tbilisi near Vaziani and ends in the town; its length is 65 km, and the drive takes about 1 h 50 min.[^2]

It is the quicker of the two routes to the town that the tourism administration describes, and it suits drivers who are "not afraid of sharp turns, serpentines and altitude", the same article says.[^2] Three things decide whether it suits you on the day: the season, the weather and how well you and your passengers cope with a mountain road.

The sections below take them in turn: when to choose the detour, what happens in winter, and how to look after yourself on the way.

### Gombori or the road via Sagarejo and Gurjaani? {#gombori-or-detour}

Take the pass when it is open and clear; take the detour when it is not. The road round the mountains via the two towns "adds an extra 40 minutes to the trip", according to the tourism administration.[^2]

We give no length and no road number for the detour: we found no official figure for either.

Choose the longer road when:

- the road authority has announced restrictions on the pass (see [winter](#gombori-winter));
- there is fog or snow on the mountain;
- you or a passenger gets carsick on bends;
- you would be crossing after dark.

**Our tip.** Decide on the morning of the trip, not the night before. Check the restrictions page first, then look at the mountains: if the tops are in cloud, the extra 40 minutes are well spent.

<!-- схема: img/gombori-pass-winter.webp | Craft paper illustration: a winding mountain road with hairpin bends between snow-dusted trees, a small dark green car on the road and snowy peaks behind | The road over the pass is open all year, but fog and snow can come from the middle of autumn -->

### Is the road over Gombori open in winter? {#gombori-winter}

Yes: the tourism administration says the road is open all year.[^2] But "in the middle of the autumn, the way may be covered by a milky fog or snow", it adds,[^2] and then the Roads Department of Georgia can restrict traffic. Check its [restrictions page](https://georoad.gov.ge/en/restriction) on the day you travel.[^7]

The department publishes restrictions as a list by date. When we checked on 20 November 2026, its most recent entries did not mention the Vaziani–Gombori–Telavi road.[^7] That is a snapshot at the time of checking, not a forecast for this winter.

Past winters show what to expect:

- **3 January 2026.** Ice on the Verona section of the pass road, from km 36 to km 64: "Only high-clearance vehicles (with two or more driving axles) are allowed to travel on this section", Interpressnews reported.[^9]
- **14 January 2026.** Heavy snowfall on the same section: only vehicles with four-wheel drive could pass, 1TV reported.[^10]
- **19 January 2026.** Heavy snow again: only off-road vehicles were allowed on that section, Georgia Today reported.[^11]
- **17 February 2021.** Poor visibility: on km 1–62 of the road, only off-road vehicles were allowed, the Ministry of Internal Affairs said.[^12]

<!-- схема: img/gombori-winter-limits.webp | Infographic: four past winter limits on the pass road — 3, 14 and 19 January 2026 on the Verona–Telavi section and 17 February 2021 on km 1–62 — with a reminder to check the Roads Department restrictions page | In January 2026 traffic on the Verona–Telavi section was limited three times in less than three weeks -->

The department's home page also carries the headline "Restoration and Stabilization of the Landslide-Damaged Section of the Vaziani–Gombori–Telavi Road Is Underway".[^8] It gives no dates and no location, so we cannot say which stretch is affected or for how long.

The UK government's travel advice warns that "snow at higher altitudes can affect roads and bridges making travel difficult or impossible" and that "landslides are common".[^13] We found no official rule on snow chains or winter tyres for this route. Before a winter drive, read the section [Night driving, mountains and winter](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/driving-in-georgia/#night-winter) in our guide to driving in Georgia.

The department lists a hotline on its site: 032 2 31 30 76.[^7]

### Motion sickness, stops and calling for help on the pass {#gombori-health}

A mountain road with many bends can make passengers carsick; our page [Motion sickness on mountain roads](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/marshrutka-georgia/#motion-sickness) has practical advice. If there is an accident or someone becomes seriously unwell, dial 112. The emergency service lists phones without a SIM card among the ways to reach it, but the phone must be within a mobile operator's coverage area.[^16]

**Our tip.** Tell the operator you are on the pass road from Tbilisi and name the nearest village or the last one you passed. Glance at village signs as you drive, so you have an answer ready. If your phone shows no network where you are, the call cannot get through, so move to where it does.

Summer brings its own risks on the mountain. The UK government's travel advice says: "Lightning and wildfires are a risk in summer, particularly in the mountains."[^13]

**Our tip.** In summer, check the weather for the mountains before you set off, and if you see smoke near the road, turn back rather than drive on.

For a wider look at road and personal safety, see [Is Georgia safe?](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/is-georgia-safe/)

## Telavi in summer: weather, heat and how to stay well {#summer-heat}

We found no official climate data for the town, so we give no temperatures here. Summer is when the NHS advice on heat below matters most, along with knowing how to call for help.

<!-- схема: img/telavi-heat-signs.webp | Infographic: signs of heat exhaustion and heatstroke from the NHS, and when to call the emergency number in Georgia | In Georgia, the emergency number is not 999 or 111: one number covers the whole country -->

For summer figures for Tbilisi and the rest of the country, see [Georgia in summer: heat, UV and storms](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/best-time-to-visit-georgia/#summer) in our guide to the best time to visit.

Most of what there is to see here means time outdoors: the fortress, the plane tree, the monasteries and the drives between them. Plan the day around the hottest hours rather than against them, and carry water from the start.

### Telavi weather by season {#weather-by-season}

We give no forecast: check one the day before you travel. Here is what our sources say about the seasons:

- **Summer.** In the mountains, lightning and wildfires are a risk.[^13]
- **Autumn.** From the middle of autumn, fog or snow may cover the pass.[^2] On 27 September every year the royal monastery nearby holds its church holiday, Alaverdoba,[^4] a good fixed date for an autumn visit.
- **Winter.** Snow can bring restrictions on the pass; see [winter on the road](#gombori-winter).

If you are choosing dates, check the pass for a winter trip and plan around the midday hours for a summer one.

### Heat exhaustion and heatstroke: the signs {#heat-signs}

Heat exhaustion is a warning; heatstroke is "a medical emergency", says the NHS.[^14] Knowing the difference tells you whether to cool down or to call for help.

Signs to watch for, according to the NHS, include:[^14]

- tiredness;
- dizziness;
- a headache;
- feeling sick;
- heavy sweating;
- cramps;
- intense thirst;
- a high temperature;
- feeling irritable;
- pale and clammy skin.

If someone has them, the NHS says to move them to a cool place, take off any unnecessary clothing, cool their skin with water or a fan, and give them plenty of water to drink.[^14]

Get emergency help, the NHS says, if the person has signs of the more serious condition, including:[^14]

- no improvement after 30 minutes in a cool place;
- hot skin that is not sweating;
- a very high temperature;
- a fast heartbeat;
- fast breathing or shortness of breath;
- confusion;
- a fit (seizure);
- loss of consciousness.

The NHS page gives UK numbers. In Georgia, dial 112 instead of 999 or 111. How the call works is explained [below](#call-112).

### Sightseeing in the heat {#heat-tips}

Do your walking early and late. The NHS advises people to "avoid the sun between 11am and 3pm" and to "drink more cold drinks".[^14]

<!-- схема: img/telavi-summer-shade.webp | Craft paper illustration: a small old town street in summer with carved wooden balconies, a big plane tree, a drinking fountain and two people resting on a bench in the shade | The NHS advises staying out of the sun between 11am and 3pm -->

**Our tip.** See the fortress and the old plane tree in the morning, while it is cooler, and visit the monasteries in the first half of the day. Keep water in the car for the drive over the pass. Travelling with children or older relatives? Plan more breaks in the shade.

More ideas for hot days: [Batumi: staying healthy in the summer heat](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/batumi/#heat) and [David Gareja: heat, sun and wildfires](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/david-gareja/#heat).

## Medical help in Telavi: hospital, clinics and 112 {#medical-help}

In an emergency, dial 112 and ask for an ambulance; the UK government's travel advice puts it in exactly those words.[^17] For hospital care, the town has a regional hospital on the tourism administration's official list.[^18]

Below: how the emergency call works, where the hospital is, what other help there is, and what your insurance should cover.

<!-- схема: img/telavi-medical-help.webp | Infographic: where to get medical help in Telavi — 112, Telavi Referral Hospital and the 1505 health hotline | Calls to the emergency number are free from any phone, landline or mobile -->

### Calling the emergency number from the town or the road {#call-112}

Use one number for all emergencies. It is Georgia's single emergency number, set up in 2012, according to [the service itself](https://112.gov.ge/?p=1686).[^15]

You can speak English. Operators take calls in Georgian, English, Azerbaijani, Armenian, Turkish and Russian, 24/7 across the whole country, according to [the emergency service](https://112.gov.ge/?p=1715).[^16] Phones without a SIM card can call it too, but the service says the phone must be within a mobile operator's coverage area.[^16] The tourism administration says the call is free from any phone, landline or mobile, on any network.[^18]

**Our tip.** Say where you are first: the street or a landmark in town, or, on the road, the last village you passed. Then say what happened and how many people need help. Stay on the line until the operator ends the call.

What the operator asks and what happens next: see [Ambulance in Georgia](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/ambulance-georgia/).

### Is there a hospital in Telavi? {#telavi-hospital}

Yes. Telavi Referral Hospital is on the tourism administration's list of regional hospitals, at V. Sekhniashvili St 1.[^18]

We do not give a phone number for it: on that list the same number appears next to several hospitals. In an emergency, call the emergency number rather than the hospital.

The Ministry of Health runs a hotline, 1505, available around the clock, according to the same page.[^18]

Keep expectations realistic. The UK government's health advice says: "Outside Tbilisi, medical facilities are limited."[^17]

For something that is not an emergency, such as a fever, a sore throat or a check after a fall, the town also has clinics. The [catalogue of clinics in Telavi](https://100doc.ge/en/telavi/) listed 13 clinics when we checked on 20 November 2026.

For the rest of the region, see [Medical help in Kakheti](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/sighnaghi/#health).

### Travel insurance for Kakheti {#insurance}

To enter Georgia you need health and accident cover, and for the trip itself it should also pay for treatment on the spot and medical evacuation.[^17][^19]

Since 1 January 2026, tourists entering Georgia must have health and accident cover of at least 30,000~GEL, under [Resolution No. 602 of the Government of Georgia](https://matsne.gov.ge/ka/document/view/6728816).[^19] Some travellers, such as holders of diplomatic passports, are exempt.[^19] What the rule asks of you is explained in [Insurance to enter Georgia (2026 rule)](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/#entry-insurance).

Meeting the entry rule does not by itself mean your travel insurance covers everything you need. Read the policy wording before you go, especially if you plan to drive over the pass in winter.

## Things to do in Telavi and nearby {#things-to-do}

In town, see Batonis Tsikhe, the fortress with the royal palace, and the giant plane tree; nearby are the monasteries of Alaverdi, Ikalto and Shuamta and the old royal capital.[^1][^3][^4][^5]

<!-- схема: img/batonis-tsikhe.webp | Craft paper illustration: fortress walls and a palace behind them, with a huge plane tree beside the walls | The palace inside the fortress dates from King Archil's reign in the 17th century -->

We found no official opening hours or ticket prices for these places, so we give none. Check on the spot.

**Our tip.** Split the sights into two half-days: the town in the morning, when it is cooler, and the monasteries on another morning. In summer this keeps you out of the worst of the heat.

### Batonis Tsikhe and the palace of King Erekle II {#batonis-tsikhe}

The fortress stands in the centre of the town. The tourism administration calls it "one of the most important monuments of secular Georgian architecture from the late feudal period".[^3]

It was built in two stages:[^3]

- **Under King Archil, in the 17th century:** the palace, the east gate and the bath.
- **Under Erekle II:** the court church, the west gate and the wall.

Inside there is a museum on the history of the kings.[^3] The tourism administration calls Erekle II "one of Georgia's most beloved kings" and says he died in his palace at the age of 78.[^1]

### The giant plane tree {#plane-tree}

It stands in the centre of town, and the tourism administration calls it "the 900-year-old plane tree".[^1] The age is the administration's figure; we found no independent dating.

**Our tip.** Pair it with the fortress on a morning walk, and keep to the shade as the day warms up.

### Alaverdi Monastery {#alaverdi}

It is a royal monastery from the 11th century, a 30-minute drive from the town, according to the tourism administration.[^4]

Its St George's Cathedral is "over forty metres tall, including the dome", the same page says.[^4] Every year on 27 September the monastery holds its church holiday, Alaverdoba.[^4] There is also a vineyard on its grounds with 80 grape varieties.[^4]

<!-- схема: img/alaverdi-monastery.webp | Craft paper illustration: a tall stone cathedral among vineyards, with mountains behind it | Alaverdoba is held at the monastery every year on 27 September -->

**Our tip.** In summer, come in the morning: otherwise both the drive and the visit fall in the hottest hours of the day.

### Ikalto Monastery and its school {#ikalto}

This monastery (Iqalto) was a centre of education in the region in the 11th and 12th centuries, says the tourism administration, which lists it as another nearby landmark.[^1]

We found no official distance or visiting details, so ask locally how to get there and when it is open.

### Gremi: the old royal capital {#gremi}

This was the capital of the Kingdom of Kakheti "for a whole century", according to the tourism administration's page on the fortress.[^5]

Its Archangel Church was built in 1565 by King Levan.[^5] The site lies near Kvareli, a 15 to 20-minute drive from the town.[^6]

**Our tip.** Carry water, and in summer plan the visit for the morning, when it is cooler.

### Shuamta monasteries and qvevri wine {#shuamta-wine}

The tourism administration lists the old and the new monastery here among the sights near the town.[^1] We found no official details on visiting them.

This is also wine country: the tourism administration says qvevri winemaking is "a method used for more than 8,000 years".[^1] For cellars, tastings and grape varieties, see [Kakheti wine: qvevri, Rkatsiteli and Saperavi](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/sighnaghi/#wine).

If you have been tasting, do not drive over the pass. Read [Driving after wine](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/sighnaghi/#driving) first.

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

### Is Telavi the same as Tel Aviv?

No. This town is in eastern Georgia, the country in the Caucasus.[^1] The city in Israel only has a similar name.

### Where is Telavi and what country is it in?

It is in Georgia, in the Kakheti region in the east of the country. The tourism administration calls it the region's cultural center.[^1]

### How far is Telavi from Tbilisi?

About 1 h 55 min by road, according to the tourism administration.[^1] The pass road, which starts from Tbilisi near Vaziani, is 65 km long.[^2] We found no official figure from the city centre.

### How do I get from Tbilisi to Telavi?

By car or taxi over the pass. We found no official timetable or fare for buses, so ask your hotel on the day. A slower road round the mountains adds about 40 minutes.[^2]

### Is the Gombori Pass open in winter?

Yes, it is open all year, but fog and snow can come from the middle of autumn.[^2] Traffic may then be limited to off-road vehicles,[^11][^12] so check the road authority's restrictions page on the day.[^7]

### How hot does Telavi get in summer?

We found no official temperatures for the town, so we give none. Avoid the sun between 11am and 3pm and drink more cold drinks, the NHS advises.[^14] Learn the warning signs in the heat section above: overheating can become a medical emergency.[^14]

### Is there a hospital in Telavi?

Yes: a regional referral hospital at V. Sekhniashvili St 1, on the tourism administration's list.[^18] In an emergency, call the single emergency number.[^15]

### Is Telavi worth visiting?

For history and wine country, yes. The tourism administration says King Erekle II died in his palace here and puts the town's plane tree at 900 years old.[^1] The royal monastery at Alaverdi is a 30-minute drive away.[^4]
