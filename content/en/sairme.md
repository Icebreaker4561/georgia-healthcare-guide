## What Sairme is and where it is {#resort}

It is a mountain spa in western Georgia, set in a forested river gorge. Georgia's [national tourism site](https://georgia.travel/resorts/sairme) puts it this way: "Sairme, or the Land of Deer, is tucked away within the conifers and deciduous trees of Baghdati Municipality."[^1]

**The region.** The [Imereti tourism organisation](https://kutaisi.travel/en/5428/resort-sairme-en/) calls it "a mountainous resort of Imereti". It adds that the place "is famous for its mineral waters and beautiful nature".[^2]

**Altitude.** The national tourism site puts Sairme resort at 950~m above sea level.[^1] The [European Historic Thermal Towns Association](https://historicthermaltowns.eu/?p=4459) gives the same figure.[^4] Wikipedia gives 915~m.[^3]

**The gorge.** According to [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sairme_%28water%29), the springs lie "in the Tsablarastsqali river gorge (the left feeder of the Khanistsqali) on the northern slope of the Meskheti Range".[^3]

**The legend.** The national tourism site retells a local story. Some brothers followed red-tinged water back to its source and found that it came from a wounded deer.[^1] Later, by the site's account, it turned out that the place "was abundant in deer, which led to it being named 'Sairme'".[^1]

**The "magical ground".** In the 1890s, the same site tells, shepherds came upon a patch of earth where snow never settled and rain evaporated in seconds.[^1] People called it "magical ground".[^1] The answer came only "in the 1920s when geologists" found "hot mineral water deep below the ground".[^1] The site gives no year for the opening of the spa, and neither do we.

**Other spa towns.** This is one of several spa resorts in Georgia. For radon baths, see our [Tskaltubo guide](https://guide.100doc.ge/tskaltubo/). For Borjomi water and how to read its label, see our [Borjomi guide](https://guide.100doc.ge/borjomi/#mineral-water).

## Sairme mineral water: drinking at the springs {#mineral-water}

Anyone can drink the mineral water for free, says the Imereti tourism organisation.[^2] A course of treatment is set at the resort's polyclinic, according to the national tourism site.[^1]

**Warm water to bathe in.** For a Sairme spa day, the national tourism site lists warm thermal water to bathe in alongside the drinking waters.[^1] The Imereti tourism organisation also names an outdoor pool.[^2]

### What the official sites say

Both official sites describe several different waters, each with its own use. Neither gives a daily amount or a schedule.

**Four drinking waters.** The national tourism site speaks of "warm thermal water to bathe in and four different kinds of mineral water to drink - each with different properties".[^1]

**The buvettes.** In the words of the Imereti tourism organisation: "A comfortable construction of mineral water buvettes is arranged near the springs, each spring has its purpose."[^2] The travel blog [Wander-Lush](https://wander-lush.org/visit-sairme-baghdati-things-to-do/) writes: "Numbered concrete buvettes dot the edge of the Bostania river in the centre of town."[^5]

**Not all the same.** Wikipedia gives a mineralisation from 1.6~g/L to 9.5~g/L; the top figure is for one borehole.[^3] So the waters are far from identical.

**Health claims.** The tourism site speaks of therapeutic effects;[^1] we found no medical confirmation. Mineral water is not a medicine.

### How much can you drink?

Large amounts without a prescription are not recommended. The Imereti tourism organisation puts it this way:

> "Everyone can drink mineral water for free, however, it should be noted that taking large amounts without a doctor's prescription is not recommended."[^2]

It gives no figure for "large amounts", and we do not invent one.

**Our tip.** If you have high blood pressure, a heart condition or another long-term illness, or keep to a low-salt diet, check with your own GP before the trip. Watch the sodium: how to read it on a bottle label is explained in our [Borjomi guide](https://guide.100doc.ge/borjomi/#sodium).

### Who decides on a course

The polyclinic on site does, and each course is set individually.

**The polyclinic.** The national tourism site describes "a 24-hour polyclinic where experienced doctors individually prescribe courses of mineral water treatment".[^1] We could not check its hours ourselves.

**Our tip.**

- Bring a list of your medicines and a short medical summary, in English if you can.
- Mention any condition, even one that is under control.
- The local physician decides. If the answer is no, accept it.

## How to get to Sairme {#getting-there}

By road it takes 1 hour 20 minutes from Kutaisi (1:20 on the national tourism site).[^1] The distance is about 50–55~km: the Imereti tourism organisation gives 50~km,[^2] Wikipedia 55~km.[^3]

### Kutaisi to Sairme

Take a marshrutka to the nearest town, then a taxi for the last stretch. A 2024 post by the travel blog Wander-Lush says: "Marshrutka vans to Baghdati depart from Kutaisi Central Bus Station throughout the day, taking around 1.5 hours to reach the town."[^5]

**The last stretch.** The blog adds: "There is no public transport to Sairme, so you'll need to take a local taxi the rest of the way."[^5] We found no official timetable, so we give none.

How the vans work is in our guide [Marshrutka in Georgia](https://guide.100doc.ge/marshrutka-georgia/). For the city where you start, see our [Kutaisi guide](https://guide.100doc.ge/kutaisi/).

### Tbilisi to Sairme

It is a long day on the road. The national tourism site gives 4:25 by road from Tbilisi and 3:20 from Batumi.[^1]

We give no prices or timetables: they change, and we found no official source for them.

### The mountain road

The road up to the resort is paved; the road beyond it, towards Abastumani, is not, according to Wander-Lush.[^5]

**From the city.** The blog puts Sairme "just over an hour's drive" south of the city and calls the road "fully paved and easy to navigate".[^5]

**Further south.** "The road after the resort between Sairme and Abastumani to the south is unpaved and very rough," the blog warns.[^5] It also says the Zekari Pass is normally closed in winter, roughly from December to March, because of heavy snow.[^5] Read [Night Driving, Mountains and Winter](https://guide.100doc.ge/driving-in-georgia/#night-winter) in our Driving in Georgia guide.

**Our tip.** Agree the ride back with your driver before you set off. Do not drive the mountain road in the dark or in snow.

## Sairme weather: when to go {#when-to-go}

Summers are moderately warm and winters mild, according to Wikipedia.[^3]

**The figures.** Wikipedia writes: "Summers are moderately warm with an average July temperature of 17 °C" and "winters are mild with an average January temperature of –1 °C".[^3] It adds: "Precipitation totals 940 mm (37.01 in) annually."[^3] It names neither the weather station nor the years.

**The season.** The adventure attractions open "roughly from the end of April until October", according to Wander-Lush.[^5] We give no live forecast here.

**Our tip.** In winter, ask your host about the road and what is open before you travel.

## Things to do in Sairme {#things-to-do}

Drinking at the buvettes, forest walks and an adventure park. Bigger sights are elsewhere; the national tourism site gives no distances to them.

**The adventure park.** The Imereti tourism organisation lists "an 800-meter Zip Line", "a city of ropes, and an outdoor pool".[^2]

**Walks.** Wander-Lush writes: "There are several marked hiking trails that start from Sairme Resort, including the 'Air Temple' trail."[^5] It gives no lengths. Before a longer walk, read our guide [Hiking in Georgia](https://guide.100doc.ge/hiking-in-georgia/).

**Further afield.** The national tourism site lists these top attractions on the same page:[^1]

- [Okatse Canyon](https://guide.100doc.ge/martvili-canyon/#okatse);
- Prometheus Cave and Sataplia Cave, both in our [Tskaltubo guide](https://guide.100doc.ge/tskaltubo/);
- [Borjomi-Kharagauli National Park](https://guide.100doc.ge/borjomi/#national-park);
- the [Katskhi Pillar](https://guide.100doc.ge/katskhi-pillar/).

**Our tip.** In the forest, watch where you step: the UK government warns of vipers, especially in early summer.[^6] Keep away from dogs, and if one bites or scratches you, see [Rabies in Georgia](https://guide.100doc.ge/rabies-georgia/). More in [Snakes, Dogs and Weather](https://guide.100doc.ge/hiking-in-georgia/#wildlife).

## Doctors and medical help near Sairme {#health}

In an emergency, dial 112. For a clinic or a hospital, plan on Kutaisi.

**Emergency.** The UK government's [health advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/georgia/health) says: "Dial 112 and ask for an ambulance."[^6] It also warns: "Outside Tbilisi, medical facilities are limited."[^6] Georgia's [112 service](https://112.gov.ge/?p=1715) says its call-takers handle calls in Georgian, English, Azerbaijani, Armenian, Turkish and Russian.[^7] It adds that to call 112 the "phone must be in the mobile operator coverage area".[^7] Step by step: [Ambulance in Georgia](https://guide.100doc.ge/ambulance-georgia/).

**At the resort.** The polyclinic is described above. The national tourism site does not say what emergencies it handles.[^1]

**Rabies and snakes.** The UK government lists "rabies – treatment is available in Tbilisi and major towns".[^6] It also lists "venomous snakes (vipers), particularly in early summer – few medical facilities outside major towns have anti-venom".[^6] After a dog bite or scratch, get medical attention immediately, the UK government advises.[^6]

**City clinics.** The catalogue of [doctors and clinics in Kutaisi](https://100doc.ge/en/kutaisi/) on 100doc.ge listed 139 clinics on 22 November 2026. Of these, 25 had a hospital profile.

**Your cover.** Visitors need health and accident cover of at least 30,000 GEL for their whole stay; see [Insurance to Enter Georgia (2026 Rule)](https://guide.100doc.ge/#entry-insurance).

**Our tip.**

- Bring enough of your regular medicines for the whole trip, in their original packs.
- Save 112 and your host's number in your phone.
- If you feel unwell in the thermal water, get out and tell the staff at once.

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

### Where is Sairme?

In Imereti, western Georgia, in a forested mountain gorge. The national tourism site puts it about 950~m above sea level.[^1]

### How do I get to Sairme from Kutaisi?

Take a marshrutka to the nearest town, then a taxi. The travel blog Wander-Lush says there is no public transport for the last stretch.[^5]

### Is the mineral water free to drink?

Yes. Everyone can drink it for free, says the Imereti tourism organisation.[^2] The buvettes stand near the springs.

### How much Sairme water can I drink?

The Imereti tourism organisation gives no figure. It says taking large amounts without a doctor's prescription is not recommended.[^2] If you limit salt or have a long-term illness, ask your GP first.

### Is there a doctor at the resort?

According to the national tourism site, a 24-hour polyclinic on site prescribes individual courses of treatment.[^1] In an emergency, dial 112.

### What is the weather like in winter?

Mild, according to Wikipedia: the January average is –1~°C.[^3] Ask your host about the road and what is open before you go.

### What is there to do in Sairme?

Drink at the buvettes and walk the marked trails.[^5] There is also a city of ropes and an outdoor pool.[^2] The adventure attractions open roughly from the end of April until October, the blog says.[^5]
