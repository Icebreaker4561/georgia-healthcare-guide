## Where is Abastumani, Georgia, and how high is it? {#where}

Abastumani resort lies at about 1,300 m,[^1] in Adigeni Municipality, in the Samtskhe-Javakheti region of Georgia, on the southern slope of the Meskheti Range, in the gorge of the Otskhe River.[^3] The Georgian National Tourism Administration calls it "Abastumani Mountain & Balneological Resort".[^9]

<!-- схема: img/abastumani-resort-forest.webp | Illustration: a valley with a winding river, conifer-covered slopes and four small houses, the setting of Abastumani | By the Georgian Encyclopedia's figures, the town is 24 km from Adigeni and 28 km from the nearest railway station -->

The administration's regional page[^10] and the Georgian Encyclopedia, a reference work,[^3] give 1,340 m instead.

## Is the altitude a problem? Abastumani and your health {#altitude}

For most visitors, probably not: Abastumani (about 1,300–1,340 m) and the hilltop astronomy site above it (about 1,650–1,700 m) are below the NHS and CDC thresholds, by our reading.

Neither the NHS nor the CDC writes about this town. The NHS says the illness comes on at heights of "usually more than 2,500 metres above sea level".[^5] The CDC gives its advice from 9,000 feet, about 2,750 m.[^11]

As a precaution, not a rule for this height, the CDC adds: "People with heart or lung disease should talk to a doctor who is familiar with high-altitude medicine before their trip".[^11]

NHS symptoms include headache, feeling sick, dizziness and difficulty sleeping; it advises rest at the same height.[^5] If it is serious, [call the emergency number](#call-112).

Higher places carry a risk the UK government names:[^8] see [Altitude sickness in Gudauri, Kazbegi and Tetnuldi](https://guide.100doc.ge/gudauri-altitude/#altitude-sickness).

## A 19th-century health resort: what is claimed and what is known {#resort}

Its fame came from mountain air and baths; official tourism pages present this as history. We found no modern clinical evidence for the climate.

The tourism administration's page recalls that reputation ("The clean mountain air has been said to work miracles by tuberculosis specialists.").[^1]

In the Georgian Encyclopedia's account, Grand Duke George Romanov (1871–1899), ailing with tuberculosis, came to Abastumani, which "by that time already enjoyed the fame of a health resort". He died there in 1899.[^12]

## Abastumani hot springs: are there sulfur baths? {#hot-springs}

There are springs, but no official source lists baths open to visitors. The regional page of georgia.travel speaks of "sulfur-rich waters",[^10] the Encyclopedia of "sulfate-chloride".[^3]

<!-- схема: img/abastumani-springs.webp | Infographic "Abastumani hot springs: what sources say about the springs", four cards: Tourism administration — waters used to treat rheumatic, gynecological, dermatological and nervous diseases (a claim, georgia.travel); Georgian Encyclopedia — three springs, Goliati, Gveli and Suravandi, 39–48 °C (a reference work, not a health authority); Not found — official water temperature, list of open baths, contraindications; Heart condition? The town page writes that a city named Odzdrakhe, famed for its springs, once stood here, citing the 18th-century historian Vakhushti Batonishvili. Today, it says, the waters are used to treat rheumatic, gynecological, dermatological and nervous diseases, among others.[^1]

The Encyclopedia, a reference work, names three thermal springs, Goliati, Gveli and Suravandi, at 39–48 °C.[^3] No official tourism or medical source gives a temperature.

**Our tip.** Ask locally which baths are open, their hours and their rules.

### Hot water and your heart: who should ask a doctor first {#who-should-ask}

Anyone with a heart condition should ask first; we found no official Georgian list of who should avoid the baths here.

The British Heart Foundation (BHF), a heart charity, writes: "we would always advise people with cardiovascular concerns to speak to their GP or cardiac nurse before using hot tubs". For peripheral arterial disease, it adds, "being in hot water for long periods can cause blood pressure to drop", with possible dizziness.[^6]

**Our tip.** With a heart condition, high blood pressure, pregnancy or daily medicine, check with your physician before the trip. Keep baths short, stand up slowly and skip alcohol beforehand. More in [Are sulfur baths safe? Who should take care](https://guide.100doc.ge/tbilisi-sulfur-baths/#health).

## Abastumani Observatory: can you visit? {#observatory}

Usually yes, with a guide, but two official pages disagree: check first.

<!-- схема: img/abastumani-observatory.webp | Illustration: three cream observatory domes on a green hill, conifer forest and a starry sky | The tourism administration's Georgian page mentions a digital museum project under way -->

The Georgian Encyclopedia says it was founded in 1932 in the town and moved to nearby Mount Kanobili in 1937; its founder, E. Kharadze, ran it until 1993.[^13] Today it is the Evgeni Kharadze Georgian National Astrophysical Observatory.[^14]

The town page says it "has been in use since 1932 and was the first astrophysical mountain observatory in the former Soviet Union".[^1] It stands at about 1,650–1,700 m (1,650 m in the Encyclopedia,[^13] 1,700 m on the Georgian-language page[^2]).

**Visiting.** The English page on georgia.travel says visitors can see a small museum and use the telescope to observe the Moon and Saturn.[^1] Its Georgian page says that night viewing through the telescope is currently suspended and that visitors tour the site only with an astro-guide, for about an hour and a half (our translation).[^2] Check before you go.

**Our tip.** Nights at the domes are colder: bring warm clothes even in summer, for children too.

## How to get to Abastumani: by car or by minibus via Akhaltsikhe {#getting-there}

By car, allow about 4–4.5 hours from the capital: the observatory's Georgian page on georgia.travel gives 240 km to the hilltop domes, via the S1 highway towards Gori, then Khashuri (our translation).[^2]

<!-- схема: img/abastumani-route-map.webp | Infographic "Roads to Abastumani, schematically", not to scale: from Tbilisi, 240 km, about 4–4.5 h — S1 highway to Gori, Khashuri (turn off here), new road to Abastumani; from Kutaisi, 100 km, about 3.5 h — Baghdati, Sairme, road works (Kakaskhidi–Zekari rebuilt 2025, 17 km bypass road due autumn 2027); by bus — no direct service, minibus to Akhaltsikhe (from Kutaisi, 1–2 a day), then change or taxi, 28 km; before you go — check road status, emergency 112 | From Kutaisi, the minibus to Akhaltsikhe runs only once or twice a day: check times and book a seat, the tourism administration says -->

**By minibus.** There is no direct service: minibuses go only to Akhaltsikhe,[^2] 28 km away.[^3] Then change or take a taxi.[^2] See [What official advice says](https://guide.100doc.ge/marshrutka-georgia/#official-advice).

The regional page describes a road south from Borjomi to that town,[^10] also the base for [Rabati Castle in Akhaltsikhe](https://guide.100doc.ge/vardzia/#rabati) and [Getting to Vardzia](https://guide.100doc.ge/vardzia/#getting-there). See [Tbilisi to Borjomi: getting there](https://guide.100doc.ge/borjomi/#getting-there).

### From Kutaisi via Baghdati and Sairme {#zekari}

The same Georgian page calls the route via Baghdati and Sairme the most convenient from Kutaisi: 100 km, about 3.5 hours (our translation).[^2] Roadworks run until autumn 2027.[^4]

In November 2018, the public broadcaster 1TV reported, citing official data, that the Zekari Pass was closed to all traffic because of heavy snow.[^15]

On the Kutaisi route, the 16 km Kakaskhidi–Zekari section was rebuilt in November 2025.[^16] A 17 km bypass road linking Abastumani with Baghdati municipality is due in autumn 2027, the public broadcaster reported in August 2026.[^4]

We found no official schedule of closures. Check the Roads Department of Georgia before you set out. More on Sairme in [How to get to Sairme](https://guide.100doc.ge/sairme/#getting-there).

### Driving in winter and at night {#winter-road}

Drive in daylight, with proper tyres or chains in the cold months. The advice below is for the whole country, not this town.

The UK government (FCDO) advises care, especially at night: lighting is often poor outside city centres, stray livestock can be a hazard and landslides are common.[^17]

The Government of Canada adds that in mountainous areas in the cold season you must have snow tyres or chains, and notes zero tolerance for drinking and driving.[^18]

## Abastumani weather: when to go {#when-to-go}

For mild conditions, go in summer. The Georgian Encyclopedia describes a moderately humid climate, with long, snowy winters and warm summers; it is a reference work, not the weather service.[^3]

The regional page adds that across the region "the sky is clear for a majority of the year", and links this to the stargazing here.[^10] No official climate figures turned up; check the forecast on the day.

When the ground is white, wear sunglasses: the NHS warns that reflected sunlight "is particularly dangerous" for the eyes.[^19]

## Forests, walks and sights nearby {#nearby}

The town lies in evergreen woodland,[^1] but we found no official walking routes around it. In the wider municipality, the municipality page names Goderdzi Fossil Forest, the 14th-century Zarzma monastery and Chule waterfall.[^9]

For a national park, see [Borjomi-Kharagauli National Park](https://guide.100doc.ge/borjomi/#national-park). Before a walk, read [Snakes, dogs and weather](https://guide.100doc.ge/hiking-in-georgia/#wildlife).

### Ticks in the forest {#ticks}

Take precautions in long grass. The Government of Canada warns of a risk of Crimean-Congo haemorrhagic fever in Georgia, though it "is generally low for most travellers", and advises checking yourself for ticks after time outdoors.[^18] No official source covers this area specifically.

WHO notes there is no widely available vaccine and advises long sleeves and trousers, approved repellent on skin and clothing, and regular checks, removing any you find safely.[^20] If you fall ill after a bite, get medical help; if it is serious, call the [emergency number](#call-112).

## Medical help near Abastumani: 112 and Akhaltsikhe {#medical-help}

In an emergency, ring the national emergency line. The UK government's advice warns: "Outside Tbilisi, medical facilities are limited."[^8] We found no official description of a hospital in the town or nearby.

### Calling 112 in Abastumani {#call-112}

One number covers every emergency, and you can speak English. It has been the single emergency number since 2012, and operators take calls in Georgian, English, Azerbaijani, Armenian, Turkish and Russian.[^7] The UK government advises: "Dial 112 and ask for an ambulance."[^8]

**Our tip.** Start with "Abastumani, Adigeni municipality". Then say where: in the town, at the domes on Mount Kanobili, or where on the route you are. More in [What to tell the operator](https://guide.100doc.ge/ambulance-georgia/#what-to-say).

### Where to find a doctor {#doctor}

For routine care, the [catalogue of clinics in Akhaltsikhe on 100doc.ge](https://100doc.ge/en/akhaltsikhe/) covers a town 28 km away, by the Encyclopedia's figure. We found no official list of medical practices in Abastumani itself.

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

### How high is Abastumani?

About 1,300 m by the tourism administration's town page, 1,340 m by its regional page; the observatory stands at about 1,650–1,700 m.[^1][^10][^13][^2]

### Can you visit the Abastumani Observatory?

The tourism administration describes guided visits; its Georgian-language page says telescope viewing is currently suspended. Check before you go.[^1][^2]

### Are there sulfur baths in Abastumani?

There are thermal springs; no official source gives their temperature or lists open baths.[^1][^3]

### Is Abastumani good for your health?

Its fame is historical; we found no modern clinical evidence. It lies below the usual mountain-sickness height; with heart or lung disease, ask a physician first.[^1][^5][^11]

### How do I get to Abastumani from Tbilisi?

About 240 km and 4–4.5 hours by car to the observatory, via Gori and Khashuri, by georgia.travel's Georgian page; there is no direct bus.[^2]

### Is the road to Abastumani open in winter?

We found no official schedule. The Zekari Pass to the north was shut to all vehicles in November 2018; check the Roads Department before you set out.[^15]

### Is there a hospital in Abastumani?

We found no official information about one. Clinics are listed in a town 28 km away. In an emergency, dial 112.[^3][^7]
