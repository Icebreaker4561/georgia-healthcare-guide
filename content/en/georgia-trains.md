## Train routes in Georgia: what the Georgian Railway schedule lists {#routes}

Fast services run from the capital to four western towns, electric ones run on several other lines, and one international route goes to Armenia. That is what the [Traffic General Schedule](https://www.railway.ge/en/traffic-general-schedule/) on railway.ge showed when we checked it on 21 November 2026.[^1]

The table gives each route, its numbers and how often it runs. The first number is the outbound service, the second the return.

| Route | Train numbers | How often |
|---|---|---|
| Tbilisi–Batumi | Fast 802/801, 804/803 | Daily |
| Tbilisi–Zugdidi | Fast 870/869 | Daily |
| Tbilisi–Poti | Fast 874/873 | Daily |
| Tbilisi–Ozurgeti | Fast 854/853 | Every second day |
| Tbilisi–Borjomi | Electric 618/617, 686/685 | Daily |
| Kutaisi–Batumi | Electric 682/681, 684/683 | Daily |
| Batumi–Ozurgeti | Electric 613/614 | Daily |
| Kutaisi I–Sachkhere | Electric 633/634 | Daily |
| Tbilisi–Yerevan | 371/372 | Every second day |

Suburban lines also run to Gardabani, between Khashuri and Zestafoni, and to Rioni.[^1]

**Times and fares** are not in this guide: they change from one timetable to the next. Look them up on railway.ge before you travel.

## Tbilisi to Batumi train and back {#tbilisi-batumi}

Two fast services run each way every day: 802 and 804 leave the capital.[^1] On the way back, the Batumi to Tbilisi train is 801 or 803, the odd number of each pair.[^1]

For the city itself, see [Batumi, Georgia: Beach, Weather by Month and Health Guide](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/batumi/).

### Tbilisi to Batumi train schedule: how many trains a day? {#tb-schedule}

Two in each direction, and the operator marks all four "Stadler-kiss".[^1] That is the whole list for this route on the day we checked.

**Journey time.** We print no figure: it changes, and railway.ge lists it for every train.[^1]

**Extra summer runs.** We could not confirm any. Plan around the two daily pairs.

**On board.** We found no official description of the carriages, so we promise no particular comforts. Bring what you need for the whole ride (see [what to take](#pack)).

### Other ways to Batumi {#other-ways}

From the capital, 802 and 804 are the only direct trains to Batumi that the operator lists. The electric pairs 682/681 and 684/683 do not start there (see the table).[^1] By road or by air, other pages of this guide have the details.

- For road options, and what official advice says about shared minibuses, see [Marshrutka in Georgia: Minibus Safety, Trains and Taxis](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/marshrutka-georgia/#alternatives).
- To fly into the west of the country, see [Tbilisi Airport Arrival: Border, Insurance, Bus and Taxi](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/tbilisi-airport/#other-airports).

**Our tip.** Buy as soon as sales open for your date, 20~days ahead. Have a back-up plan in case your service is full or cancelled.

## Beyond Batumi: the west, the mountains and trains abroad {#other-routes}

Away from the coast route, the main lines go across the west of the country, into the mountains and over the border to Armenia. Each is covered below.

### Is there a Tbilisi to Kutaisi train? {#kutaisi}

Not a direct one: we found none when we checked on 21 November 2026.[^1] That can change, so look again before you travel.

The Kutaisi to Batumi train runs as two electric pairs, 682/681 and 684/683, and 633/634 runs to Sachkhere.[^1] For the road and other ways between the two cities, see [Kutaisi, Georgia: Bagrati, Gelati, Airport and Health Guide](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/kutaisi/#getting-there).

### Is there a Tbilisi to Borjomi train? {#borjomi}

Yes: two electric services run every day, 618/617 and 686/685.[^1] For the town and the road, see [Borjomi, Georgia: Mineral Water, Park and Sulfur Pools Guide](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/borjomi/#getting-there).

From there, there is also a narrow-gauge line to Bakuriani. Before you plan on it, read [Is the Kukushka train running?](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/bakuriani/#kukushka)

### Is there a Tbilisi to Yerevan train? {#international}

Yes: 371/372, every second day.[^1] The [international page](https://www.railway.ge/en/passenger-traffic/international-trains/) names Georgian Railway and Armenian Railway.[^4] It runs overnight.[^1]

Its stops, as that page lists them, start in the capital and run through Marneuli, Sadakhlo and Vanadzor to Yerevan.[^4] We give no times, fares or classes.

Heading east? [Georgian Railway](https://www.railway.ge/en/traffic-general-schedule/) lists no Tbilisi–Baku train. The UK government's travel advice, though, says there is a daily train service between Georgia and Azerbaijan.[^1][^6] Check the current service before you plan around it.

## Train stations in the capital and Batumi: what they are called {#stations}

The names people search for are not the ones the operator uses. Travellers often search for Tbilisi Central Station, a name we did not find on the operator's site. Its international page lists two stops in the capital: "Tbilisi – passenger station" and "Tbilisi-junction".[^4]

For the coast, people look for Batumi railway station. We found no official name or address for it on the operator's site, and no addresses for the others either.

**Our tip.** Save your station's name on your phone, spelt as railway.ge spells it. Show it to your driver when you take a cab there.

## How to buy Georgian Railway tickets {#tickets}

At ticket offices, at pay boxes, online or in the mobile app. The [passenger rules](https://www.railway.ge/en/passenger-traffic/) say sales open up to 20~days before departure. They stop 10~minutes before the train leaves, "for the safety of passengers".[^2][^3]

The rules name the offices and the pay boxes, but we did not find where the offices are.[^2] The sections below cover where to buy, what ID you need, and refunds.

### Online, in the app or at the station? {#where-to-buy}

Any of them works: the rules list all four.[^2]

**Our tip.** To buy online, start from the "Internet ticket" link on railway.ge rather than from a search result.

**Wheelchair users.** People who use wheelchairs can buy online through tkt.ge, the operator's [information for passengers](https://www.railway.ge/en/information-for-passengers/) says.[^3]

**Fares and classes.** Fare and class options are shown when you buy. We did not find them as text on the operator's site, so we quote none.

### What ID do you need for a train ticket? {#documents}

A personal identification number to book, and an identity document to board.[^2] For children under 14, the rules ask for a birth certificate or passport, if they have one. You are let on board only after showing your ticket and that document.[^2]

**Foreign visitors.** We did not find what a visitor without a Georgian personal number enters instead. Ask the operator before you book.

**Our tip.** Keep that document at hand for the whole ride, not in a bag on the rack.

### Refunds, delays, children and pets {#refunds}

You get the full price back if you return the ticket early enough, and part of it later.[^2] Here are the rules:

- **Refunds.** The full price no later than 15~hours before departure; 85~% between 15 and 4~hours; 70~% with less than 4~hours to go. Only the person named on it can return it, with their ID. A lost one is not replaced.[^2]
- **Delays.** Under the [delay compensation rules](https://www.railway.ge/en/delay-repay-compensation/), you can claim at least 25~% back for a delay of 60 to 119~minutes. For 120~minutes or more, it is at least 50~% of the price.[^5] That page explains how to claim.
- **Children.** Under 5 they travel free without their own seat; from 5 to 10 they need a child fare; from 10, adult rules apply.[^2] With more than one child under 5, check the rules before you book.
- **Pets.** Under the [rules for small pets](https://www.railway.ge/en/rules-for-travelling-with-small-pets-and-birds-by-train/), small animals travel free in a container if they take no seat, with a veterinary certificate.[^11] The rules give the container as 55x35x20 without units, so check before you go.

## Health on the train: heat, water, medicines and emergencies {#health}

Conditions differ from line to line. In its [travel advice](https://travel.gc.ca/destinations/georgia), the Government of Canada says the condition and cleanliness of trains "vary greatly from route to route".[^7] Below: what to bring, how to keep your things safe, and when to call for help.

### Is it hot on the train in summer? {#heat}

We cannot say: we found no official information on cooling in the carriages, so we promise neither a cool ride nor a hot one. Know the warning signs instead.

**The signs.** The UK's [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/) lists the signs of heat exhaustion: tiredness, dizziness, a headache, intense thirst, feeling sick, heavy sweating with pale, clammy skin, and cramps.[^8]

**What to do.** Move the person somewhere cooler, take off extra clothing, give plenty to drink, and cool their skin. Stay with them.[^8]

**When it is an emergency.** "Heatstroke is a medical emergency," the NHS says.[^8] Its signs of heatstroke include a very high temperature, hot skin that is not sweating, a fast heartbeat, fast breathing, confusion, a seizure or loss of consciousness. Call an ambulance if you see them, or if the person is no better after 30~minutes of rest in a cool place.[^8] The NHS gives the UK number, 999; in Georgia, dial 112 ([see below](#call-112)). While you wait for help, the NHS says to wrap the person in a cool, wet sheet, or fan them or sponge them down with cold water.[^8]

On arrival at the coast, see [Staying healthy in the summer heat](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/batumi/#heat).

### What to take for a long train ride {#pack}

**Our tip.** Pack for the whole journey, not for the next stop:

- Drinks and food for the full ride. GOV.UK notes that water quality varies in Georgia.[^10]
- Your medicines in your hand luggage, in their original packaging. See [Bringing Medication to Georgia: Rules, Codeine, Pharmacies](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/bringing-medication-to-georgia/#ten-packs).
- A charged phone with the emergency number saved.

If winding stretches make you queasy, see [Motion sickness on mountain roads](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/marshrutka-georgia/#motion-sickness).

### Is the train safe? Your bags and the compartment {#safety}

Take ordinary care with your things. The UK government's [travel advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/georgia/safety-and-security) on rail says to "take usual precautions with your personal safety and belongings". It notes that compartments on sleeper services usually have locks.[^6]

The Government of Canada's advice adds: "Don't leave your compartment unattended" and "Keep the compartment door locked from the inside". It also asks you to keep your passport and other travel documents secure at all times.[^7]

For road travel, see [Is Georgia Safe to Travel in 2026?](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/is-georgia-safe/#roads).

### Feeling unwell on the train: calling 112 {#call-112}

Call the emergency number yourself. [Georgia's emergency service](https://112.gov.ge/?p=1715) says it has been the single emergency number since 2012. It answers 24/7 across the whole country, and its call-takers speak six languages, English among them.[^9]

The UK government's [health advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/georgia/health) puts it simply: "Dial 112 and ask for an ambulance."[^10] It also warns: "Outside Tbilisi, medical facilities are limited."[^10]

**Our tip.** Ask the conductor to help. Before you set off, note your train number from the operator's timetable, for example 802. Tell the call-taker that number, your direction (towards the coast or the capital) and the last station you passed. More in [What to tell the operator](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/ambulance-georgia/#what-to-say).

**If it can wait.** For a problem that can wait until you arrive, see the [catalogue of doctors and clinics in Batumi](https://100doc.ge/en/batumi/) on 100doc.ge.

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

### Are there trains in Georgia, the country?

Yes. Fast and electric services run from the capital to the coast and the west, and an international one goes to Armenia.[^1]

### How do I buy train tickets in Georgia?

At ticket offices, pay boxes, online or in the app, up to 20~days ahead. Sales stop 10~minutes before departure.[^2][^3]

### What is the Tbilisi to Batumi train price?

Fares change, so we do not quote them. The current fare is shown when you buy.

### How many trains a day go to Batumi from the capital?

Two fast ones each way, 802/801 and 804/803, daily, as checked on 21 November 2026.[^1]

### Is there a train that goes directly from Kutaisi to Tbilisi?

Not when we checked on 21 November 2026.[^1] Check again before you travel.

### Do I need my passport on the train?

You board only with your ticket and an identity document, so keep it to hand.[^2]

### What number do I call in an emergency on a train?

112. Call-takers answer 24/7 and speak English.[^9]
