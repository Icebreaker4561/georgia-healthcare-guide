> [!facts] In short
> - Don't flag cars down in the street: book a ride through an app and check that the seatbelts work.[^1]
> - Bolt lists ten Georgian cities, including Tbilisi, Batumi and Kutaisi;[^2] Yandex Go has a page for the country;[^3] Uber's list of countries leaves Georgia out.[^4]

> - If there is no meter, agree the price before you set off and keep small notes ready.[^6]
> - After a crash, call 112, the single emergency number, answered 24/7.[^7]

> - Airport ride to the centre: 20–30 min

> - Bus 337 to the centre: 1 GEL

This page is about Georgia the country, not the US state, and is part of the [Georgia Travel Guide](https://guide.100doc.ge/). Checked on 22 November 2026.

## Taxi apps in Georgia: Bolt, Yandex Go and what about Uber {#apps}

No need to hail a car: to get a taxi in Georgia, use a ride app. Bolt lists ten Georgian cities,[^2] and Yandex Go has a page for the country.[^3] The Government of Canada notes that "Ride-sharing services are also popular throughout Georgia."[^6]

### Bolt in Georgia: which cities {#bolt}

Bolt in Georgia covers ten cities on its [list of cities](https://bolt.eu/en/cities/): Bakuriani, Batumi, Gori, Gudauri/Stepantsminda, Kutaisi, Poti, Rustavi, Tbilisi, Telavi and Zugdidi.[^2]

**In the capital.** Bolt's city page promises a ride "within minutes".[^8] Its categories include Taxi Tbilisi, described as "Ride in bus lane", and Economy.[^8]

**Flying in.** The service's list of airports includes Tbilisi, Kutaisi and Batumi.[^9]

**Paying.** Bolt's pages we checked don't say which payment methods work here.

**Our tip.** Before you confirm the ride, check the category, the price and the payment method on the screen.

### Yandex Go in Georgia: what its page says {#yandex-go}

Yandex Go's English page for the country says you can "request rides and order item delivery in Georgia". It adds: "Pay with cash or by card."[^3]

The page names no cities, so check whether the service covers the place you are in before you rely on it.

### Is there Uber in Georgia? And Maxim? {#uber-maxim}

Georgia is not on Uber's list of countries,[^4] so plan on another service. Maxim's country page does not list it either;[^10] we could not confirm that the service works here.

## How to take a Tbilisi taxi safely, step by step {#how-to-ride}

Pre-book a ride and never stop a car in the street. The UK government's [travel advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/georgia/safety-and-security) says "Taxi provision and safety has improved significantly" and lists three rules: "do not flag taxis in the street", "try to pre-book taxis through an app" and check for "working seatbelts".[^1]

Whatever taxi service in Tbilisi you choose, the same steps apply:

1. **Book.** Use an app. At a hotel, the Government of Canada's advice is to "ask for your taxi at the front desk if you’re using hotel services".[^6]
2. **Our tip.** Before you get in, make sure the car and the plate match the booking.
3. **Price.** Agree it first: "negotiate the fare in advance if there is no meter".[^6]
4. **Belt.** Check that the belt works before the car moves off.[^1]
5. **Pay.** Keep change ready: "have small bills available for payment".[^6]

More on the capital in [Is Tbilisi Safe Day and Night?](https://guide.100doc.ge/is-georgia-safe/#tbilisi)

### How to recognise an official taxi in Tbilisi {#official-taxi}

[The Government of Canada's travel advice](https://travel.gc.ca/destinations/georgia) gives two signs of an official taxi: in Georgia, taxis "are regulated and are recognizable by their white colour and roof-mounted taxi signs".[^6] It asks travellers to "use only officially marked taxis".[^6]

**Unofficial cars.** The same advice warns: "Unofficial taxis continue to operate, and drivers generally don’t use meters. They may overcharge you for the ride."[^6]

**Our tip.** If a car has no roof sign and you didn't book it, order a ride instead.

## Airport taxis: Tbilisi, Kutaisi and Batumi {#airport}

All three international airports list taxi services at the terminal, but only the capital's publishes a fixed fare.[^5][^11][^12]

### Tbilisi Airport taxi: the fixed fare {#tbilisi-airport}

The fixed rate is 40 GEL to the city centre and 50 GEL to suburban areas, according to [the airport's site](https://www.tbilisiairport.com/en/passengers/to-from-the-airport/page/taxi), checked on 22 November 2026.[^5] Cars wait "outside the terminal, just at the curbside of the Arrival Hall" with a "24-hour service", and the ride takes "20-30 minutes, depending on traffic".[^5] The page gives no date for the fares.

**Booking online.** Bolt's page for the capital offers to "Request a ride to and from Tbilisi airports".[^8]

**Booking ahead.** The Government of Canada's advice is to "arrange your pickup with a reputable company before arrival when travelling by air, or use authorized cabs at the airport".[^6]

**The bus.** Bus 337 costs 1 GEL;[^13] times and stops are in [Tbilisi Airport to the City](https://guide.100doc.ge/tbilisi-airport/#to-the-city).

**Our tip.** Ignore offers of a ride inside the arrivals hall: walk out to the kerb or book a ride.

### Kutaisi: the airport taxi and Bolt {#kutaisi}

Kutaisi International Airport's site lists one taxi operator, working 24/7 in the arrivals hall,[^11] and three bus transfer operators, also 24/7.[^14] It gives no prices.

**Distance and rides.** The airport lies 14 km west of the city, according to Bolt.[^15] Its Kutaisi page lists XL Travel, "Large vehicles with seating for 6", and Bolt Travel, "comfortable intercity rides".[^15] Economy is not listed there, so check the app for rides in town. More in [Arriving at Kutaisi Airport](https://guide.100doc.ge/kutaisi/#airport).

### Batumi: taxis at the terminal exit {#batumi}

In the words of Batumi airport's site: "Taxis are available right upon the exit of the terminal; it is approximately 10-15 minute drive to the city centre."[^12] Municipal bus No10 runs on a fixed route, Airport–Batumi Centre.[^12]

**Ride categories.** Bolt's Batumi page lists XL Travel, with seating for 6, and Economy.[^16]

## Taxi prices in Tbilisi and how to pay {#fares}

The only official price we found is the fixed airport rate above.[^5] Ride services don't publish prices on the pages we checked, so check the price in the app before you confirm.

**Paying.** Payment methods for the apps are covered above. With a driver you hire directly, keep small notes ready.[^6]

**Lari and cards.** For money in general, see [Cash or Card in Georgia?](https://guide.100doc.ge/money-in-georgia/#cash-or-card)

**Our tip.** Settle the amount in lari, not dollars or euros, and say the address before the car moves off.

## Taxi between cities: Kutaisi, Batumi, Kazbegi and Yerevan {#intercity}

We found no official fare for trips between cities, so agree it before you set off.[^6] Bolt's Kutaisi page lists Bolt Travel, "comfortable intercity rides".[^15]

**Roads.** In the Government of Canada's words, "Roads are generally in good condition in Tbilisi and in major cities, but in rural areas, they are sometimes poorly maintained" and can be dangerous.[^6] More in [Roads, Taxis and Marshrutkas: the Bigger Risk](https://guide.100doc.ge/is-georgia-safe/#roads).

**Our tip.** Avoid long drives after dark on mountain roads, and plan the way back before you leave. If the driver goes too fast, ask them to slow down or stop.

**Other ways.** Trains and minibuses are alternatives on some of these routes: [Tbilisi to Batumi Train and Back](https://guide.100doc.ge/georgia-trains/#tbilisi-batumi), [Tbilisi to Kazbegi: the Georgian Military Road](https://guide.100doc.ge/kazbegi/#getting-there), [Tbilisi to Borjomi: Getting There](https://guide.100doc.ge/borjomi/#getting-there) and [Marshrutka in Georgia](https://guide.100doc.ge/marshrutka-georgia/#alternatives). In the capital, the [Tbilisi Metro](https://guide.100doc.ge/tbilisi-metro/) is the alternative to a car.

## Safety in the car: seatbelts, the back seat and night rides {#safety}

The UK government's advice for the car itself is to "check the taxi has working seatbelts".[^1]

**Our tip.** Wear the belt in the back seat too.

### Seatbelts and travelling with children {#seatbelts}

We found no official rule on child seats in hired cars that we could check.

**Our tip.** If you travel with a small child, ask the service about a child seat before you book, or bring your own.

### The back seat and travelling alone {#back-seat}

The Government of Canada's advice is to "Use reputable taxi services or ride-sharing services and sit in the back seat".[^6] It also asks travellers to "never share a taxi with strangers".[^6] More in [Is Georgia Safe for Solo Female Travellers?](https://guide.100doc.ge/is-georgia-safe/#women)

**If you feel threatened.** In its safety advice, the UK government says: "If someone attempts to assault you or you feel threatened, call the emergency number 112 and ask for the police."[^1]

### Taxis at night {#night}

The UK government's general advice: "Take usual precautions after dark in unfamiliar areas."[^1]

**Our tip.** At night, book through the app, send your route to a friend and wait for the car indoors.

## After a crash or if you feel unsafe: call 112 {#call-112}

Dial 112 at once: it is Georgia's "single emergency number", and its call-takers handle calls 24/7 for the police, fire and rescue and the ambulance.[^7]

**What to say.** Start with where it happened, "the exact location of the incident".[^7] More in [What to Tell the Operator](https://guide.100doc.ge/ambulance-georgia/#what-to-say).

**In English.** The Government of Canada's advice mentions "the 112 Georgia mobile application to contact emergency service providers in English".[^6] No SIM card, or can't talk? See [No SIM Card, No Credit on the Phone or Can't Talk](https://guide.100doc.ge/ambulance-georgia/#no-sim-app).

**Driving yourself?** See [What to Do After a Road Accident](https://guide.100doc.ge/driving-in-georgia/#accident).

If anyone is hurt, call the emergency number first.

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

### Which taxi app works in Tbilisi?

Bolt lists the capital among its ten cities in the country,[^2] and Yandex Go has an English page for Georgia.[^3]

### Is there Uber in Tbilisi?

Not as far as we can tell: Uber's list of countries does not include Georgia,[^4] and Maxim's country page does not list it either.[^10]

### What cities does Bolt cover in Georgia?

Bakuriani, Batumi, Gori, Gudauri/Stepantsminda, Kutaisi, Poti, Rustavi, Tbilisi, Telavi and Zugdidi.[^2]

### How much is a taxi from Tbilisi Airport to the city centre?

A fixed 40 GEL to the centre and 50 GEL to suburban areas, as listed on the official taxi page (checked 22 November 2026); the ride takes 20–30 minutes.[^5]

### Is it cheaper to book a car in advance or on arrival?

We can't compare: ride services don't publish prices on the pages we checked, while the taxi rate at Tbilisi Airport is fixed.[^5] Official advice lists both options: pre-book with a reputable company or use authorized cabs on arrival.[^6]

### How much is a taxi from Kutaisi to Tbilisi?

We found no official price for this trip. Whichever way you book, agree the fare before you set off.[^6]

### Is it safe to take a taxi in Georgia?

The UK government says taxi safety "has improved significantly".[^1] Its advice: don't flag cars in the street, pre-book a ride and check the seatbelts work.[^1]

### What number do I call after a taxi accident?

112, the single emergency number, answered 24/7; its page lists the police, fire and rescue and the ambulance.[^7]
