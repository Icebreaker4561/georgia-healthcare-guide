## Where is Chiatura, and is it worth visiting? {#where}

It is a town in Imereti, western Georgia, 340–500 m above sea level.[^1] It is worth a day if you want to ride the cabins over the town and see the rock column nearby; plan transport and the way back before you set off.

## Chiatura cable cars: the old Soviet lines and the new ones {#cable-cars}

Two systems share the name. The Soviet lines stopped carrying passengers in 2016, the ministry says,[^2] and the municipality reports a new system running from August 2021.[^3]

### What happened to the Soviet cable cars? {#old-lines}

They were taken out of passenger service. The ministry says that for three decades their cables and gondolas "cannot meet modern standards and are dangerous for travelling", and that passenger traffic stopped in 2016.[^2]

**How big the system was.** The Imereti Regional Administration writes, in the past tense: "There were 17 cableways for the passengers and 21 ropeways for manganese."[^8] It also dates the first cableway to 1948.[^8] The municipality gives 1954 for the first passenger line in the USSR, between the town centre and the Perevi industrial area.[^1]

**Lines that stopped earlier.** In 2019 the municipality reported that the Tkhelidze line had stood idle since 1999 and the one on Abashidze Street since 2003. Both stations were completely worn out, specialists said that keeping them in that state was dangerous, and both lines were to be dismantled.[^9]

**Today.** We found no official confirmation that any old line carries passengers, and the builder of the new system says most of the old ones are abandoned.[^10] Do not treat any old station as open.

### The new cable cars: lines, price and hours {#new-lines}

A ride costs 0.5 GEL, and we found no official hours. The Chiatura cable car price is set by [Chiatura Municipal Council (Sakrebulo) Decision No. 3 of 22 January 2024](https://matsne.gov.ge/ka/document/view/6081081/0) for travel on the municipal line in all directions.[^4] That is the price at the time of checking; we did not check later amendments.

**How many lines.** The builder, Poma, lists four: Sanatorium, Lezhubani, Naguti and Mukhadze, with eight cabins for 15 people each and a height difference of up to 180 m.[^10] In 2017 the ministry listed three lines under construction and a fourth in planning.[^11] No official list says which of them run today.

**Our tip.** Before you plan the day around a line, ask at the station whether it is running. The town hall's "Hot line", a municipal line rather than a medical one, is +995 599 271 247, from 09:00 to 18:00.[^1]

### Are the Chiatura cable cars safe? Closures and what to do {#cable-car-safety}

For the old lines, official sources answer plainly: the ministry calls their cables and gondolas dangerous for travelling,[^2] and in 2019 the municipality wrote that specialists considered the worn-out stations dangerous.[^9] For the new lines, we found no official data on inspections, stops in bad weather, or health and age limits.

The builder says the new cabins are linked to a control post and have night lighting.[^10]

**Our tip.**

- Ride only the working cabins at the new stations.
- Keep away from derelict stations and old cabins, and never go inside them.
- If a line is closed, do not wait under the cable: ask at the station.
- Afraid of heights, or prone to motion sickness? Try one short ride first.
- Keep children beside you, at the station and in the cabin.
- If something goes wrong, call [the emergency number](#call-112).

## Katskhi Pillar: where it is and can you climb it? {#katskhi}

It is a rock column about 40 m high by the village of Katskhi, which Chiatura Municipality calls the "Katskhi column".[^6] The Georgian National Tourism Administration says ordinary visitors may not climb it,[^5] while the municipality's undated page says anyone can, so plan to see it from the ground.[^6] Katskhi village also has a working church you can enter freely, the tourism administration says;[^12] the climb, both churches and the road are covered in full in our [Katskhi Pillar](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/katskhi-pillar/) guide.

## Near Chiatura: Mgvimevi Monastery and other trips in Imereti {#nearby}

Another sight in the area lies in the Kvirila gorge, reached by a long staircase from the town.

### Mgvimevi Monastery: the long stairs and the tunnel {#mgvimevi}

It is a 13th-century monastery complex in the Kvirila River gorge, by the village of the same name, also spelled Mghvimevi. Both the municipality and the regional administration give that date.[^6][^8]

The municipality describes a two-nave basilica with carved decoration on its facade, a bell tower and a cave part. A long, narrow staircase leads from the town to the site, and from it you can go into a tunnel.[^6]

**Our tip.** The stairs are long and narrow: wear good shoes and take water. With small children, or with heart or knee problems, judge your strength first. In rain, expect slippery steps.

**More of the region.** For other day trips, see [Tskaltubo: Radon Baths, Sanatoriums, Sataplia and Health](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/tskaltubo/) and [Okatse Canyon: the hanging trail](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/martvili-canyon/#okatse).

## How to get to Chiatura from Kutaisi and Tbilisi {#getting-there}

Drive, or use a shared van; both are covered below. From Kutaisi to Chiatura it is 72 km by the municipality's figure,[^1] so the town works as a day trip if you start early.

From Tbilisi to Chiatura we quote no road distance: the municipality gives a figure without naming the route, and the regional administration gives another, by railway.[^1][^8]

### By car: driving there and back {#by-car}

Driving is the simplest option, because the way back is in your hands. The UK government's [travel advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/georgia/safety-and-security) for Georgia warns that "roads outside city centres are often badly lit and poor quality" and that "stray livestock can be a hazard".[^13] It asks drivers to "take care when driving, especially at night".[^13] More in [Georgia roads: what to expect](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/driving-in-georgia/#roads).

**Our tip.** Drive there and back in daylight, and set off early enough that the return does not fall after dark.

### By marshrutka, and getting back {#marshrutka}

Unofficial sources describe shared vans from the regional centre and from the capital, but we found no official timetable, fare or stop, or confirmation that a passenger train runs. Plan the way back before you leave.

Many shared vans in the capital leave from around Didube metro station; see [Didube: where many minibuses leave from](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/marshrutka-georgia/#where).

The Government of Canada's travel advice says: "Mini-buses called 'marshrutka' are poorly maintained and often lack security features like seatbelts. Avoid using them."[^14]

**Our tip.** Do not count on a late van back: agree a taxi for the return in advance. If bends make you feel sick, read [Motion sickness on mountain roads](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/marshrutka-georgia/#motion-sickness) first.

### Chiatura weather and landslides on the road {#weather-road}

The regional administration describes a humid subtropical climate, with cold winters and a relatively dry, hot summer.[^8] We leave out its figures, which the page gives in a garbled form: check the forecast on the day you travel.

The UK government's travel advice for Georgia warns that "heavy rain, flooding and snow at higher altitudes can affect roads and bridges" and that "landslides are common".[^13] It adds that "lightning and wildfires are a risk in summer, particularly in the mountains".[^13]

Current traffic limits are on the Roads Department of Georgia's [restrictions page](https://georoad.gov.ge/en/restriction).[^15] For the seasons, see [Three climates in one small country](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/best-time-to-visit-georgia/#regions).

## Medical help in Chiatura: 112, hospitals and insurance {#medical-help}

In an emergency, dial 112 and ask for an ambulance; the UK government's [health advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/georgia/health) puts it in exactly those words.[^16] The same advice warns: "Outside Tbilisi, medical facilities are limited."[^16]

### Calling 112 in Chiatura {#call-112}

One number covers every emergency, and you can speak English. The [emergency service](https://112.gov.ge/?p=1715) says it has been the single emergency number since 2012 and takes calls 24/7 across the whole country, in Georgian, English, Azerbaijani, Armenian, Turkish and Russian.[^7] The phone must be in a mobile operator's coverage area, it adds.[^7]

**Our tip.** Say where you are first: "Chiatura, Imereti". Then name the place: a cable car station or line, the pillar, the stairs in the Kvirila gorge, or the road. On the road, give your direction: towards Kutaisi or towards Tbilisi. If your phone shows no network, move to where it does. More in [What to tell the operator](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/ambulance-georgia/#what-to-say).

### Where is the nearest hospital? {#nearest-hospital}

We cannot say which is nearest: we found no official address for one in Chiatura. The tourism administration's medical page lists two hospitals in the regional centre, a referral and a central one, and none in the town.[^17]

The UK government's list of medical facilities in Georgia, updated 16 October 2026, has none in this region either: all are in the capital and Batumi, and the list says it is not exhaustive.[^18]

For care there, see [Hospitals and doctors in Kutaisi](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/kutaisi/#health) or the [catalogue of doctors and clinics](https://100doc.ge/en/kutaisi/) on 100doc.ge.

**Your cover.** Georgian law requires visitors to hold health and accident cover of at least 30,000 GEL for their whole stay, under [Resolution No. 602 of the Government of Georgia of 26 December 2025](https://matsne.gov.ge/ka/document/view/6728816).[^19] More in [Insurance to enter Georgia (2026 rule)](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/#entry-insurance). The UK government warns that you or your insurer "will need to pay for all treatment as it's provided".[^16]

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

### Is Chiatura worth visiting?

Yes, if you want to ride the new cabins over the town and see the rock column nearby. Plan transport and the way back before you go.

### Where is the Katskhi Pillar?

By the village of Katskhi, west of Chiatura. The municipality gives the column's height as 40 m.[^1][^6]

### Can you climb the Katskhi Pillar?

Plan to see it from the ground. The tourism administration says ordinary visitors may not go up;[^5] the municipality's undated page says anyone can.[^6]

### Do the Chiatura cable cars still run?

The new ones have run since August 2021, the municipality reports,[^3] but we found no later official confirmation of which lines run. The old lines stopped carrying passengers in 2016, the ministry says.[^2]

### How much is a cable car ticket in Chiatura?

Half a lari (50 tetri) a ride, under the Council Decision of January 2024.[^4]

### How to get to Chiatura without a car?

Unofficial sources describe shared vans, but we found no official timetable, and the Government of Canada advises avoiding marshrutkas.[^14] Arrange the way back before you set off.

### What is the population of Chiatura?

Chiatura Municipality gives 39,884, without a date.[^1]
