## Where is Sachkhere, Georgia? {#where}

It lies in Imereti, in the basin of the upper and middle Qvirila and Dzirula rivers.[^1] The town is in Sachkhere Municipality.

<!-- схема: img/sachkhere-town.webp | Illustration: a small stone railway station by a river with an electric train, red-roofed houses and green hills behind | The town lies in the basin of the Qvirila and Dzirula rivers, says the national tourism site -->

**The land.** The regional administration describes the relief of the municipality as "average and low mountainous".[^6] Its page carries no date.

**A long history.** The area has been inhabited since the Bronze Age, and the municipality has 127 historical monuments, 27 of them of national significance.[^1]

**Population.** We found no current official population figure for the town, so we do not give one.

## Modinakhe Castle: the fortress above the town {#modinakhe}

The national tourism site calls the fortress of Modinakhe perhaps the most popular sight in the town.[^1] It stands 700 m above sea level on a chalk limestone ridge,[^2] about 1 km north of the town, with a panoramic view of Upper Imereti.[^3]

<!-- схема: img/sachkhere-modinakhe.webp | Illustration: fortress walls on a pale limestone ridge, a tall tower at one end, two towers at the other and a small chapel, with red-roofed houses on the slope below | Russian troops garrisoned the fortress after the uprising of 1810, says the national tourism site -->

**The layout.** According to the national tourism site:[^2]

- two roads led to the castle;
- two long walls: one ended in a high tower, and the other end was fortified by two towers;
- a house in the middle, with a chapel and a dungeon under it;
- a cellar in the yard, with qvevris buried in the ground.

**Finds and history.** Samples from both the early and the late Bronze Age have been found here. After the uprising of 1810, Russian troops occupied the fortress and placed a garrison in it.[^2]

**The legend of the name.** The story goes that Davit Abashidze refused to let the nobleman Papuna Tsereteli marry his daughter, because Papuna had no fortress. Once he had built one, he wrote: "Come and see", or *modi da nakhe* in Georgian. Both tourism sites tell this legend as the origin of the name.[^1][^3]

### Can you visit Modinakhe Castle? {#modinakhe-visit}

Yes, according to an undated page; check on the spot. The regional tourism site says the castle is under restoration but "can be visited at any time, free of charge", and lists it as "Open: Every day".[^3] The state of the works today is unknown.

**Our tip.** Go in daylight and wear closed shoes. If you find a fence or a works sign, do not go past it. Hold children's hands near the walls.

**If you slip on the path.** First aid for small knocks is in [Sprains, Blisters and When to Call 112](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/hiking-in-georgia/#injuries).

### When was Modinakhe built? The sources disagree {#modinakhe-date}

Three official sources give three answers:

- **The national tourism site** calls it an "18th-century fortress".[^1]
- **The regional tourism site** says on one page both that it was built in the 18th century and that Papuna Tsereteli built it in the 1830s.[^3]
- **The regional administration** dates it to the 17th century.[^6]

## What else to see in Sachkhere Municipality {#nearby}

Beyond the castle, the national tourism site lists a writer's house museum, a monastery and several medieval churches in the municipality.[^1]

**Akaki Tsereteli house museum.** It is in Skhvitori village and honours the "famous Georgian writer and poet", in the national tourism site's words.[^1] The regional administration calls it a state museum and also mentions an ethnographic museum in the town itself.[^6] We found no official opening hours for either.

**Jruchi Monastery.** It stands near Tskhomareti village. A manuscript of the gospels known as the Jruchi Gospel was once kept here.[^1]

**St George Church in Savane.** It was built in 1046, according to the national tourism site.[^1] The regional administration dates it to the 10th century.[^6]

**Koreti and Speti.** Koreti Church and the Church of the Savior in Speti both date from the 11th century.[^1]

## How to get to Sachkhere: train and road {#getting-there}

Without a car, take the daily electric train listed in the national railway's schedule.[^4] By car, we found no official road distance or driving time.

<!-- схема: img/sachkhere-map.webp | Schematic map of Sachkhere, Kutaisi, Chiatura and Tbilisi with the daily train line to Sachkhere | The station is in the town centre on the right bank of the river; the railway reached the town in 1904 -->

### Sachkhere train from Kutaisi {#train}

Georgian Railway's general schedule lists train 633/634 Kutaisi I–Sachkhere among its passenger electric trains, running daily.[^4] Times can change, so check the [Georgian Railway schedule on railway.ge](https://www.railway.ge/en/traffic-general-schedule/) before you go.

**The station.** It is in the centre of the town, on the right bank of the Qvirila (also spelled Kvirila), at #1 Kostava Street. The railway here dates back to 1904, when the first line was laid.[^7]

**From Tbilisi.** Whether you can reach the start of this line by rail is answered in [Is There a Tbilisi to Kutaisi Train?](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/georgia-trains/#kutaisi) Other lines: [Train Routes in Georgia: What the Georgian Railway Schedule Lists](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/georgia-trains/#routes).

**Our tip.** Ask about the return train at the ticket office while you are at the station.

### By road and marshrutka {#by-road}

Check the route in a navigation app before you set off: we found no official road distance, and no official minibus timetable either. The Government of Canada advises against using minibuses;[^9] more in [Marshrutkas: What Official Advice Says](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/marshrutka-georgia/#official-advice).

**Onward.** See [How to Get to Chiatura from Kutaisi and Tbilisi](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/chiatura/#getting-there); it does not cover the road from here.

**Our tip.** Drive there and back in daylight. Fill up and download an offline map before you leave.

## Sachkhere weather and road safety {#weather-road}

We found no official climate data for the town itself, so plan by season and road conditions. The UK government warns that "landslides are common",[^8] and the Government of Canada names Imereti among the regions where heavy rains may cause "significant flooding and landslides".[^9] The rainy season usually runs from April to July.[^9]

**On the road.** The UK government's advice notes that:[^8]

- roads outside city centres are often badly lit and poor quality;
- heavy rain, flooding and snow at higher altitudes can affect roads and bridges;
- "Take care when driving, especially at night."

**In winter.** In the mountains you must have "snow tires or chains", says the Government of Canada.[^9] More in [Night Driving, Mountains and Winter](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/driving-in-georgia/#night-winter); for the west of the country, see [Three Climates in One Small Country](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/best-time-to-visit-georgia/#regions).

**Our tip.** After heavy rain, put off mountain stretches. In the cold months, check your tyres before you set off.

## Medical help in Sachkhere: 112, ambulance and the nearest city {#medical-help}

For any emergency in the town, the number is 112. UK government [health advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/georgia/health) is plain: "Dial 112 and ask for an ambulance."[^10] The same page warns: "Outside Tbilisi, medical facilities are limited."[^10]

### Calling 112 from the town {#call-112}

The line works round the clock across the country, and operators can take your call in English or five other languages.[^5] There is also a 112 mobile app.[^9]

**Ambulance in the town.** In 2018 the regional administration reported a new ambulance centre here, with 11 rooms.[^11]

**Our tip.** Start with where you are: "Sachkhere, Imereti". Add something a crew can find: the station on Kostava Street, the road up to the castle or your village. More in [What to Tell the Operator](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/ambulance-georgia/#what-to-say).

### Is there a hospital in Sachkhere? {#nearest-doctor}

Do not count on one: we found no official record of a medical facility's address and services in the town. Call 112 and follow the dispatcher.

**A medical centre.** In 2019 the regional administration mentioned a Sachkhere Medical Center, which supported a new substitution-therapy office in the town.[^12] It gave no address or list of services.

**Rural areas.** There are shortages of medical supplies and personnel in rural areas.[^9]

**The regional centre.** For doctors in the region's centre,[^13] see [Hospitals and Doctors in Kutaisi](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/kutaisi/#health), or the catalogue of [doctors and clinics in Kutaisi](https://100doc.ge/en/kutaisi/) on 100doc.ge.

**Before the trip.** The UK government advises a policy that includes "unexpected medical evacuation".[^10] Check the entry rule as well: [Insurance to Enter Georgia (2026 Rule)](https://icebreaker4561.github.io/georgia-healthcare-guide/#entry-insurance).

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

### Where is Sachkhere?

A town in Imereti, in the basin of the Qvirila and Dzirula rivers.[^1]

### What is the town known for?

Its castle, Modinakhe, "perhaps the most popular sight", says the national tourism site. The municipality has 127 historical monuments, 27 of national significance, and the Akaki Tsereteli house museum in Skhvitori.[^1]

### Can you visit Modinakhe Castle?

An undated page of the regional tourism site says yes: every day, at any time, free of charge, despite restoration work.[^3] Look for fences or works signs on the spot.

### How old is the castle?

The sources disagree: the 17th century, the 18th century or the 1830s.[^1][^3][^6]

### How do I get there from Kutaisi?

By the daily electric train 633/634; check times in the railway's schedule.[^4] By car, use a navigation app.

### Is there a hospital in the town?

We found no official address or list of services for one. In 2018 the regional administration reported a new ambulance centre;[^11] in an emergency, dial 112.[^10]

### What is the weather like there?

We found no official climate data for the town. Heavy rains may cause flooding and landslides in this region; the rainy season usually runs from April to July. In winter, mountain drivers need snow tyres or chains.[^9]
