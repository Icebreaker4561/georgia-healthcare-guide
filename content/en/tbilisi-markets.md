## Dry Bridge Market: the Tbilisi flea market {#dry-bridge}

Dry Bridge Market is a daily flea market on a bridge in central Tbilisi, between Saarbrücken Square and the 9 April Garden.[^7] Dedaena Park is next to it, and the Presidential Palace is a short walk away.[^8][^2]

<!-- схема: img/tbilisi-markets-dry-bridge.webp | Illustration: stalls on an old stone bridge with medals, coins, old tableware, a radio and paintings on easels | More than a hundred vendors gather on the bridge every day -->

**Its history.** The market has existed in its current form since the 1950s, when locals began bringing their old items there to sell.[^2] The bridge itself dates from the mid-19th century.[^2]

**Painters.** Part of the bridge is an artists' garden, with paintings by local painters on show.[^7]

### What you'll find: antiques, Soviet items and paintings {#dry-bridge-what}

Old household objects, USSR-era memorabilia and art.

Sellers offer Second World War medals, symbols of that era, enamel engravings, silver jewellery, antique tableware, radios, ornate swords and work by local artists and craftsmen.[^2] The painters' page also lists coins and rare dishes.[^7] Vintage clothes, paintings, antique things and books are sold too.[^8] Musical instruments and, before the New Year, tree decorations are on sale as well.[^9]

Thinking of buying something old? First read [Taking antiques and Soviet items out of Georgia](#export).

### Can you haggle at the Dry Bridge? {#haggling}

Yes: vendors on the bridge barter with locals and tourists alike.[^2] Official pages give no rules for bargaining.

**Our tip.** Ask the price before you pick an item up, and count your change before you walk away.

## Dezerter Bazaar: Tbilisi's food market {#dezerter}

Dezerter, also spelled Deserters' Bazaar, is a produce market near Tbilisi's Central Railway Station, covering about 2,000 square metres.[^10] A typical day there starts around 7:00,[^10] and the official hours are 7:00–17:00 daily.[^1]

### What's sold: fruit, cheese, herbs, spices and churchkhela {#dezerter-what}

Produce that sellers bring in from the villages around the city, along with imported goods.

Stalls sell fruit, vegetables, nuts, dairy (sour cream, sulguni and cottage cheese), herbs, churchkhela, tkemali, tklapi, Georgian spices and bay leaves.[^10] For what these dishes and sauces are, see [Traditional Georgian food: what to try first](https://guide.100doc.ge/georgian-food/#dishes).

<!-- схема: img/tbilisi-markets-dezerter.webp | Illustration: market stalls with cheese, nuts, bunches of herbs, sacks of spices and strings of churchkhela | Villagers from around Tbilisi bring cheese, nuts and herbs to the market -->

### How to get to Dezerter Bazaar {#dezerter-getting-there}

Our tip: take the metro to Station Square (Vagzlis Moedani).[^11] The official market page puts the stalls near the Central Railway Station but names no metro stop or walking route.[^10]

For the platforms and transfers, see [Changing lines at Station Square](https://guide.100doc.ge/tbilisi-metro/#station-square).

## Other markets: Navtlughi and Lilo {#other-markets}

Navtlughi is a food and flower market by Samgori railway station. Lilo Market opens 8:00–17:00 daily; official pages say nothing more about it.[^1]

**At 3 Meveli Street.** Stalls sell vegetables, milk, Georgian cheeses, beef and chicken; according to the page, a quality assurance lab pre-tests and certifies the produce. There is also a herb market, a wholesale flower market from dawn to 10 am, vintage shops with copper and porcelain dishes, and small artisans who repair bags, belts and jewellery. The nearest metro is Samgori.[^12] The page gives no opening times.

## Tbilisi market opening hours and the best time to go {#hours}

The food stalls near the Central Railway Station open 7:00–17:00 daily;[^1] the bridge market runs every day, in the daytime.[^2][^8]

**Why no times for the bridge.** The tourism administration's pages give different hours for it, so we do not quote one. Check on the day.

**Getting there.** The metro runs from 06:00 to 00:00 every day.[^1] For the bridge, our tip is Freedom Square metro station (Tavisuplebis Moedani);[^11] official pages name no stop for it. By bus, plan the route in the TTC journey planner.[^13]

<!-- схема: img/tbilisi-markets-hours.webp | Infographic "When Tbilisi markets are open": a timeline from 6:00 to 24:00 with bars for Dezerter Bazaar, 7:00–17:00 daily; Lilo Market, 8:00–17:00 daily; Navtlughi flowers, dawn to 10:00; metro, 06:00–00:00; the Dry Bridge bar has no times and reads "daily, daytime, hours vary, check on the day"; sources: Georgian National Tourism Administration, TTC | The metro runs daily from 06:00 to 00:00 -->

**Our tip.** Shop for food in the morning: the market day starts around 7:00.[^10]

## Is market food safe? Fruit, cheese, meat and water {#food-safety}

Official advice comes down to clean hands, no raw meat or seafood, and care with food from street vendors.[^14]

**Hands and raw food.** The CDC advises washing hands with soap before eating, or using a hand sanitiser with at least 60% alcohol.[^14] It advises against eating raw meat or seafood, cut-up fruit and fresh salads. It also advises avoiding food from street vendors; if you do eat it, choose food cooked and steaming hot.[^14]

**Vaccine.** The CDC recommends the hepatitis A vaccine for unvaccinated travellers one year old or older going to Georgia.[^15]

**Water.** Water quality varies, and bottled water is widely available.[^16]

**Who to call.** The National Food Agency (NFA), a public body under the Ministry of Environment and Agriculture, runs a hotline on 15 01.[^17] For cheese, raw milk and food poisoning, see [Food safety rules for travellers](https://guide.100doc.ge/georgian-food/#safe-eating).

**If you feel unwell.** To find a doctor in the city, use the [catalogue of doctors and clinics in Tbilisi on 100doc.ge](https://100doc.ge/en/tbilisi/). If a life is at risk, dial 112.[^16]

<!-- схема: img/tbilisi-markets-food-safety.webp | Infographic "Buying food at the market", eight cards: wash hands or use sanitiser with at least 60% alcohol; avoid raw meat and seafood; skip cut fruit and salads made by others; street vendors: best avoided, if you eat, only cooked and steaming hot (CDC); hepatitis A vaccine recommended for unvaccinated travellers (CDC); water quality varies, bottled water is widely available (UK government); NFA hotline: 15 01; emergency: 112 | CDC advises avoiding food from street vendors; if you eat it, it should be cooked and steaming hot -->

**Our tip.** Keep wet wipes and hand sanitiser in your bag, and rinse anything you eat raw with bottled water. With children, hold hands in the crowd and carry water and wipes.

## Cash, change and pickpockets {#cash-safety}

Pay in local cash, in small notes, and keep your bag closed and in front of you.

**Money.** The lari is the only legal tender in Georgia, except in free zones, duty-free shops and some cases set by the National Bank.[^3] It has held that role since 2 October 1995.[^18] Change money at exchange offices registered with the National Bank of Georgia or at a bank.[^3][^18]

**Our tip.** Carry small notes and do not count on paying by card. Watch the scales when your food is weighed. More in [Cash or card in Georgia?](https://guide.100doc.ge/money-in-georgia/#cash-or-card)

**What governments advise.** The UK government says crime levels are low and advises sensible precautions against pick-pocketing, particularly in crowded places, and not leaving valuables unattended.[^4] The Government of Canada says: "Petty crime, such as pickpocketing and bag snatching, occurs, especially in crowded urban areas and at public gatherings." It adds that thieves may target tourists on Tbilisi buses and the metro.[^5]

Neither government names the markets. For evenings and the city overall, read [Is Tbilisi safe day and night?](https://guide.100doc.ge/is-georgia-safe/#tbilisi)

## Taking antiques and Soviet items out of Georgia {#export}

Cultural valuables need an export permit from the ministry; modern souvenirs are not covered.

Law No 985-II on the export of cultural valuables sets the rules:[^6]

- The ministry responsible for culture issues the permit. It is the basis for clearing customs, is one-off and is valid for three months (art. 28).
- The state expertise is paid for by the owner (art. 16–17).
- Only the lawful owner or their representative may export the item (art. 33).
- Cultural valuables include, for example, writings, coins and seals more than 100 years old, and furniture more than 100 years old. For other groups, such as handmade paintings and old books, the law sets no age. Modern souvenirs and mass-produced items are not included (art. 4).
- Illegal export brings liability under the law (art. 39).

Age is one test, not the only one. Medals and coins from the stalls may be new or old; if you are not sure, ask the seller and the ministry before you buy.

<!-- схема: img/tbilisi-markets-export.webp | Infographic "Taking an old item out of Georgia", a path: item may be a cultural valuable (for example coins, writings, seals or furniture over 100 years old), then state expertise paid by the owner, then export permit from the ministry, one-off and valid 3 months, then customs; on the side: modern souvenirs and mass-produced items, not covered; source: Law No 985-II, matsne.gov.ge | An export permit for cultural valuables is one-off and valid for three months -->

**Our tip.** Ask the seller how old the item is and keep the receipt. If in doubt, check with customs before your flight.

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

### Is it safe to shop at Tbilisi markets?

Pickpocketing happens in crowded places, so keep your bag closed and in front of you.[^4][^5]

### Do Tbilisi markets take cards?

Official pages do not say whether stalls take cards. Bring cash in lari, Georgia's only legal tender, in small notes.[^3]

### Is there a Christmas market in Tbilisi?

New Year tree decorations are among the goods sold on the bridge.[^9] For seasonal fairs, check the city's announcements.

### How much do things cost at the markets?

Official pages give no price list for food or old items. Ask the price before you pick something up.

### What should I buy at a Tbilisi market?

Medals and paintings on the Dry Bridge; cheese, nuts and churchkhela at the produce market by the Central Railway Station.[^2][^10]

### Can I take antiques out of Georgia?

Cultural valuables need an export permit from the ministry, valid for three months. Modern souvenirs are not covered.[^6]

### Is it safe to buy meat and cheese at the market?

Wash your hands and avoid raw meat and seafood. If you buy from street vendors, choose food cooked and steaming hot.[^14]
