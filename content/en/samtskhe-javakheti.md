> [!facts] In short
> - The Javakheti Plateau is the coldest place in Georgia in winter, according to the Agency of Protected Areas.[^1]
> - Samtskhe-Javakheti joins two historical provinces and includes a spa and a ski resort, Vardzia and Rabati Castle in Akhaltsikhe.[^2]
> - Paravani Lake lies at 2,073 metres;[^3] altitude sickness usually starts above 2,500 metres.[^4]
> - In the snowy months, cars in mountain areas must have snow tyres or chains,[^5] and an off-road vehicle is needed to reach that lake.[^6]
> - Emergency: dial 112, 24/7, with English and Armenian among its six languages.[^7] Hospitals are listed in four towns here.[^8]

> [!stats]
> - Paravani Lake: 2,073 m
> - Highest peak, Didi Abuli: 3,300 m
> - Ninotsminda: 1,940 m
> - Akhalkalaki: about 1,700 m (Encyclopedia)
> - Birds in Javakheti National Park: 140 species
> - Emergency: 112

## Where is Samtskhe-Javakheti, Georgia? Map and main towns {#where}

Pages of the Georgian National Tourism Administration place its uplands in southern Georgia.[^9][^10] The administrative region joins two provinces, Samtskhe and Javakheti.[^2] Higher up lie [the two upland towns](#plateau-towns); the main sights have their own sections below.

**People.** About 155,000 people lived here at the 2024 census.[^11]

## What to see in Samtskhe-Javakheti: the main places {#places}

Here you find old strongholds and cloisters, a cave site, spa and ski towns, and the high lakes of [Javakheti](#javakheti).

### Akhaltsikhe: Rabati Castle and Sapara Monastery {#akhaltsikhe}

Akhaltsikhe is a town in the region, with a castle complex in its old quarter.

In the upper part of the complex stand a history museum, a 9th-century church, a mosque, a madrasa and a citadel;[^12] the whole complex covers seven hectares.[^13] More in [Rabati Castle in Akhaltsikhe](https://guide.100doc.ge/vardzia/#rabati).

Its walls also enclose a synagogue and a Catholic church.[^14]

**The monastery**, spelled Safara in the title of the tourism administration's page, is still in use, in the forest near Ghrele village, 10 km from town. The administration notes it is "not an easy place to find".[^15]

<!-- схема: img/samtskhe-javakheti-akhaltsikhe.webp | Illustration: a stone fortress with towers and a domed church on a wooded hill above a town with red roofs, a river and a bridge | The complex's lower part has modern tourist infrastructure, says the tourism administration -->

### Vardzia, Khertvisi and Aspindza {#vardzia}

This 12th-century cave monastery is listed as an "Immovable Cultural Monument of National Significance".[^16] Read [Rockfalls and the 2026 closure near the Secret Tunnel](https://guide.100doc.ge/vardzia/#rockfalls) before you go.

The stronghold of Khertvisi stands on a rocky cape near the mouths of two rivers; a path leads up to it from the village.[^17] Both sites lie in the municipality whose centre is Aspindza.[^18]

### Borjomi and Borjomi-Kharagauli National Park {#borjomi}

It is a mineral-water spa town and the gateway to the park. For the water itself, see [Borjomi mineral water: what it is and who should go easy on it](https://guide.100doc.ge/borjomi/#mineral-water).

The park was the first created in independent Georgia, in 1995.[^19] Before a hike, registration at the visitor centre is required, and from October to April you may meet shepherds' dogs on the trails.[^20] Read [Snakes, dogs and weather](https://guide.100doc.ge/hiking-in-georgia/#wildlife) first.

The protected areas agency lists the Green Monastery, 8 km from town, among the sights around the park.[^19] More in [Borjomi-Kharagauli National Park](https://guide.100doc.ge/borjomi/#national-park).

### Bakuriani {#bakuriani}

It is a ski town 1,700 metres up, about 180 km from the capital; the peak season runs from December to March.[^21] See [Bakuriani ski resort: slopes, lifts and season](https://guide.100doc.ge/bakuriani/#ski-resort).

### Abastumani {#abastumani}

This mountain resort, home to an observatory, lies at 1,340 metres by the tourism administration's regional page.[^2]

## The Javakheti Plateau: lakes, national park and Poka {#javakheti}

It is a high, open upland with large lakes. The tourism administration calls it "one of the coldest and most desolate of the inhabited Georgian regions".[^22]

**The biggest water.** Paravani has the "largest surface area of any of the country's lakes", 37.5 km², according to the administration's geography page.[^23] Its own page says only "one of the largest".[^3]

<!-- схема: img/samtskhe-javakheti-plateau.webp | Illustration: a blue lake among green hills, with a few birds above the water and a snow-capped mountain behind | Only one tree species, the Caucasian pine, grows around Paravani, the tourism administration says -->

| Lake | Height | Iced over |
|---|---|---|
| Paravani | 2,073 m[^3] | thick ice from December[^3] |
| Tabatskuri | about 2,000 m (1,991 m on another page)[^24][^13] | end of December to end of March[^24] |
| Khanchali | 1,928 m[^25] | November to April[^25] |
| Kartsakhi | 1,799 m[^10] | December to mid-March[^10] |
| Saghamo | — | 4–5 months[^26] |
| Madatapa | — | November to April[^27] |

**Poka.** A monastery stands here on the shore of that 2,073-metre lake. It dates back to the 11th century, opened in 1989 and became a convent in 1992; the nuns make cheese and sweets.[^28]

**Gorelovka.** This village was settled by Doukhobors, forcibly relocated here by the Russian Empire in 1841; their old orphanage is now a museum.[^29]

### Javakheti National Park {#national-park}

Within the park lie the waters of Khanchali, Bughdasheni and Madatapa and the Kartsakhi and Sulda managed reserves; observation towers stand along its trails.[^22]

The protected areas agency counts 140 species of nesting or migrating birds, and snowshoes can be hired at the visitor centre.[^1] Didi Abuli, the region's highest peak, rises to 3,300 metres.[^22][^2] For visiting conditions, call the park on 577 64 04 82.[^1]

### Akhalkalaki and Ninotsminda: towns on the plateau {#plateau-towns}

Both sit in the uplands, above 1,600 metres.

**Akhalkalaki** became a town by the 9th or 10th century. It has places of worship from the 8th to the 11th centuries and the ruins of an 11th-century fortified town; the highest peak is a 14 km drive away.[^30] Its height is about 1,700 m: 1,710 m by the Georgian Encyclopedia[^31] and 1,620–1,725 m on a tourism administration route page.[^32]

**Ninotsminda** lies beside Khanchali, at 1,940 metres. The administration describes cold winters with little snow and long, cool summers.[^9]

## How high is Samtskhe-Javakheti? Altitude and your health {#altitude}

Places on the uplands lie at about 1,700–2,073 metres. By our reading, that is below the heights where the NHS and CDC start to warn, but within the range TravelHealthPro gives figures for.

<!-- схема: img/samtskhe-javakheti-altitude.webp | Infographic "How high is Samtskhe-Javakheti?", horizontal bars: Abastumani 1,340 m (regional page), Bakuriani 1,700 m, Akhalkalaki about 1,700 m (sources differ), Kartsakhi Lake 1,799 m, Khanchali Lake 1,928 m, Ninotsminda 1,940 m, Tabatskuri Lake about 2,000 m (1,991 on another page), Paravani Lake 2,073 m, Didi Abuli peak 3,300 m; ticks on a threshold scale below: TravelHealthPro, rarely from 1,500 m; NHS, usually above 2,500 m; CDC, from low ground not above 2,750 m in a day; note: our reading, towns and lakes lie below the NHS and CDC thresholds; heart or lung disease, ask a doctor (CDC); sources: GNTA, Georgian Encyclopedia, NHS, CDC, TravelHealthPro | Rate of ascent and sleeping height are risk factors, TravelHealthPro says -->

**What the health services say.** The NHS says altitude sickness usually comes on at "more than 2,500 metres above sea level".[^4] Symptoms include headache, feeling sick, tiredness, dizziness and trouble sleeping; if unwell, rest at the same height.[^4]

The CDC advises against going from low ground to above 9,000 feet (2,750 m) in a single day.[^33] It adds: "People with heart or lung disease should talk to a doctor who is familiar with high-altitude medicine before their trip".[^33]

TravelHealthPro notes that the illness "may rarely occur at altitudes between 1,500–2,500m".[^34] The same page adds: "Approximately 9–25 percent of unacclimatised individuals ascending to 2,000–3,000m develop AMS", that is, acute mountain sickness.[^34]

**Our reading.** None of these sources names these uplands; the conclusion is ours. The highest peak, at 3,300 m, is above all three thresholds. For higher places, see [Altitude sickness in Gudauri, Kazbegi and Tetnuldi](https://guide.100doc.ge/gudauri-altitude/#altitude-sickness).

**Our tip.** Take the first day easy, with no hard climbs. With heart or lung disease, see your physician before the trip, as the CDC advises.[^33]

## Winter on the plateau: cold, frozen lakes and how to dress {#cold}

The uplands are the coldest place in Georgia at that time of year,[^1] and the water stays frozen for months (see the table above).

<!-- схема: img/samtskhe-javakheti-lake-ice.webp | Infographic "When the Javakheti lakes freeze", headed "Javakheti Plateau: the coldest place in Georgia in winter": a calendar from November to April with ice bars for Madatapa, November to April; Paravani, from December, all winter; Kartsakhi, December to mid-March; Tabatskuri, end of December to end of March; a footnote gives Saghamo, frozen 4–5 months, dates not given; a hypothermia card: body temperature below 35 °C, shivering, pale cold skin, slurred speech, tiredness or confusion (NHS) | Saghamo, not charted, stays frozen 4–5 months, says the tourism administration -->

**Temperatures.** Akhalkalaki figures come from the Georgian Encyclopedia, a reference work, not the meteorological service. It gives a yearly mean of +4.9 °C, −7.3 °C in January and +16 °C in August.[^31]

**Hypothermia.** The NHS describes it as a dangerous drop in body temperature below 35 °C. Signs include shivering, pale and cold skin, slurred speech, tiredness or confusion. Long hours outdoors in the cold and wet clothes raise the risk.[^35]

**Snowshoeing.** At the 2,073-metre lake, snowshoes are not for hire on the spot, the tourism administration says: bring your own and hire a trained local guide.[^6]

**Our tip.** Dress in layers, with a waterproof jacket and gloves, as the tourism administration advises for ski trips.[^21] More in [Georgia in winter: Tbilisi, mountain roads and skiing](https://guide.100doc.ge/best-time-to-visit-georgia/#winter).

### Samtskhe-Javakheti weather: when to go {#when-to-go}

The region's resorts operate in all four seasons.[^2] Choose by activity:

- Skiing: the peak season runs from December to March.[^21]
- Hiking around Tabatskuri: best from May to the end of November.[^24]
- Fishing on Kartsakhi: May to October, with a hook only.[^10]

## How to get to Samtskhe-Javakheti from Tbilisi {#getting-there}

Two routes lead in from the capital.[^2] For the mineral-water spa, take the Western Highway through Gori and Khashuri. For the upland towns, the cave site and Akhaltsikhe, take the Southern Highway past Paravani,[^2] which lies 132 km from the capital.[^6]

**Two descriptions.** The tourism administration's page on that last town describes a different way, a winding route "through the beautiful Borjomi Valley", and estimates 2 hr 45 min by car.[^14]

**Driving times.** To Ninotsminda, the administration estimates 3 hr 10 min by car.[^9] For Tabatskuri, it calls the Tbilisi–Tsalka–Akhalkalaki route "much easier to drive" than the Tskhratskaro Pass from the ski town.[^24]

**Without a car.** We found no official minibus timetables here; see [What official advice says](https://guide.100doc.ge/marshrutka-georgia/#official-advice). For rail, see [Is there a Tbilisi to Borjomi train?](https://guide.100doc.ge/georgia-trains/#borjomi) The small "Kukushka" railway between the spa and ski towns is currently not operating.[^21]

### Winter roads on the plateau {#winter-roads}

Fit proper tyres or chains, and plan on an SUV for some routes.

<!-- схема: img/samtskhe-javakheti-winter-roads.webp | Infographic "Six tips for the upland roads" (Driving to the plateau in winter), six cards: snow tyres or chains in mountain areas in winter (Government of Canada); off-road vehicle to reach Paravani in winter (tourism administration); Tskhratskaro Pass, Bakuriani to Tabatskuri, choose an SUV (tourism administration); December 2023, Akhalkalaki–Kartsakhi: no trailers or buses over 30 seats (Roads Department via 1TV); check Restrictions on the Roads Department site (our tip); plan to arrive before dark (our tip) | Signs in rural areas may be in Georgian only, the Government of Canada says -->

**Government advice.** The Government of Canada's travel advice says: "If you're driving in mountainous areas in the winter, you must have snow tires or chains on your vehicle." It also says rural roads are sometimes poorly maintained, with stray livestock, few traffic signs and poor lighting.[^5] Its health section adds: "First responders may not be able to access rural areas."[^5] It covers the whole country, not this area by name.

**The tourism administration** adds that in the cold months the drive to the 2,073-metre shore needs an off-road vehicle.[^6] For the Tskhratskaro Pass route to Tabatskuri, it says to "choose an SUV".[^24] On its two-day route here, "special tires may be necessary".[^32]

**Past closures.** On 24 December 2023, a snowstorm led to a ban on trailers and buses with more than 30 seats on km 1–36 of the Akhalkalaki–Kartsakhi highway.[^36] On 25 December 2023, a section of the Akhaltsikhe–Ninotsminda highway reopened to light vehicles after clearing; the ban on trailers and large buses stayed.[^37] The public broadcaster reported both, citing the Roads Department. These are past examples, not the current status: check the Restrictions section of the Roads Department's English site before you set out.[^38]

**Our tip.** Drive in daylight, fill the tank, and keep water, warm clothes (spare layers for children too) and an offline map in the car. More in [Night driving, mountains and winter](https://guide.100doc.ge/driving-in-georgia/#night-winter).

## Hospitals in Samtskhe-Javakheti and emergency help {#medical-help}

In an emergency, dial 112. For other care, the tourism administration lists medical facilities in four towns.[^8]

- Akhaltsikhe Medical Center, Shota Rustaveli St. 105a
- Borjomi Geo Hospital, Giorgi Saakadze St. 3
- Adigeni Hospital, Balakhashvili St. 11
- Ninotsminda Hospital, Tavisupleba St. 48

For these four it gives no hours or types of care. The health ministry's hotline, 1505, works around the clock.[^8] The UK government (FCDO) warns: "Outside Tbilisi, medical facilities are limited."[^39]

For the ski town, read [Is there a hospital in Bakuriani? Where to get medical help](https://guide.100doc.ge/bakuriani/#medical-help). For routine care, use the [catalogue of clinics in Akhaltsikhe on 100doc.ge](https://100doc.ge/en/akhaltsikhe/).

### Calling 112 in Samtskhe-Javakheti {#call-112}

It is the single emergency number, working since 2012 and answering calls 24/7 across the whole country. Operators take calls in Georgian, English, Azerbaijani, Armenian, Turkish and Russian.[^7] The UK government advises: "Dial 112 and ask for an ambulance."[^39]

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

### How high is Samtskhe-Javakheti?

The upland towns lie at about 1,700–1,940 m, the biggest lake at 2,073 m and Didi Abuli at 3,300 m.[^3][^22][^9][^31][^32]

### Is it cold in Samtskhe-Javakheti in winter?

Yes. The protected areas agency calls Javakheti the coldest place in Georgia at that time of year, and its lakes freeze for months.[^1][^24]

### Are the roads open in winter?

Snowstorms have restricted traffic in the uplands before, as in December 2023. Mountain areas require snow tyres or chains, and some routes need an SUV.[^36][^5][^6]

### How do I get from Tbilisi to Akhaltsikhe?

By car, the tourism administration estimates about 2 hr 45 min.[^14]

### What is there to do in Akhaltsikhe?

See the castle, with a citadel and a mosque in its upper part, and an active forest cloister 10 km from the town.[^12][^15]

### Where is the nearest hospital?

The tourism administration lists facilities in four towns here. In an emergency, call the national number first.[^8]

### What number do I call in an emergency?

112, 24/7. Operators speak English and Russian, among other languages.[^7]

### Is Paravani the largest lake in Georgia?

By surface area, yes, according to the tourism administration's geography page: 37.5 km².[^23]
