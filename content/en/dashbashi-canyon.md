## Where is Dashbashi Canyon, and why is it also called Tsalka Canyon? {#where}

The canyon lies in the Khrami River gorge, on a lava plateau near the village of Dashbashi. Both names are official: Georgia's National Tourism Administration (georgia.travel) and the Agency of Protected Areas use them together.[^1][^5] The park gives the address as Dashbashi Village, Tsalka Municipality; the law places the canyon in the same municipality.[^3][^6]

<!-- схема: img/dashbashi-canyon.webp | Illustration: a gorge on a lava plateau with a glass bridge over it, a river at the bottom and a path with stairs down the canyon wall | The canyon is eight kilometres long, georgia.travel says -->

It is a natural monument; georgia.travel places it within Algeti National Park.[^1] The 2013 Law of Georgia on the Creation and Management of Natural Monuments (No. 1582-რს) lists it by name and gives its area as 538 hectares.[^6]

The height above sea level depends on the source: georgia.travel says 1,200–1,500 m, the Agency of Protected Areas 1,110–1,448 m.[^1][^5]

## Tbilisi to Dashbashi Canyon: the road, the way back and getting around {#getting-there}

The Agency of Protected Areas gives 92 km and 1 h 40 min from Tbilisi to the area; the park's site says about 90 minutes.[^5][^3] Go by road to the nearest big town, Tsalka, then to the village of Dashbashi and the canyon.[^1]

### How far is the canyon from Tbilisi? {#distance}

The Agency of Protected Areas gives 92 km and 1 h 40 min from Tbilisi.[^5] georgia.travel measures differently: 88 km to the nearest big town, 4 km to the village and 0.5 km from the village centre to the canyon.[^1] The Agency calls the last stretch to the village a 3 km gravel road, about 10 minutes by car;[^5] georgia.travel says 4 km.[^1]

### By car, taxi or marshrutka {#by-car-or-minibus}

We found no official timetable, stop or fare for a minibus to the canyon, so plan your own transport. The park says guided transfers and private transport are available, with parking on site.[^3]

The Government of Canada's travel advice is blunt: minibuses called "marshrutka" are "poorly maintained and often lack security features like seatbelts", and it advises avoiding them.[^7] The UK government's travel advice adds that roads outside city centres are often badly lit and poor quality.[^8]

See [Georgia roads: what to expect](https://guide.100doc.ge/driving-in-georgia/#roads) and [What official advice says](https://guide.100doc.ge/marshrutka-georgia/#official-advice).

**Our tip.** Agree on the way back before you leave: a taxi that waits, or a driver for the whole day. Set off early enough to be back in daylight, and read [Night driving, mountains and winter](https://guide.100doc.ge/driving-in-georgia/#night-winter) if you drive yourself.

## Diamond Bridge: the glass bridge over the canyon {#bridge}

The Diamond Bridge is a 240-metre bridge with a glass floor across the canyon.[^1][^3] georgia.travel adds a bar and restaurant at its centre.[^1]

<!-- схема: img/dashbashi-diamond-bridge.webp | Illustration: a long bridge over a gorge with a glass structure shaped like a diamond in the middle, cliffs and waterfalls below | The Diamond Bridge is 240 m long, according to both georgia.travel and the park -->

We give no height, opening year or cost: neither georgia.travel nor the park publishes them.

### Is the Diamond Bridge in Tbilisi? {#not-tbilisi}

No. It spans the canyon, outside the capital. The Bridge of Peace in Tbilisi is a different structure: a pedestrian bridge between Erekle II Street and Rike Park, which georgia.travel says is covered in blue glass panels.[^9]

### Fear of heights and the glass: who should think twice {#heights}

The park publishes no age, weight or health rules for the bridge.[^2][^3] "Not listed" does not mean "no limits", so ask at the desk before you buy.

The only rule we found is for the waterfall trail: visitors taller than 1.2 m and no pets. The park recommends hiking or sports shoes, says the way to the trail includes stairs, and warns that the trail may close when conditions are bad.[^3]

What follows is our advice, not official guidance.

**Our tip.**

- If you are afraid of heights, do not stop in the middle of the bridge and do not look down from the edge.
- Go with a companion who is not afraid of heights.
- Hold children by the hand and keep them away from the railing.
- If you feel dizzy or panicky, step off towards the shore, sit down and call the staff. For chest pain or if you get worse, call [the emergency number](#call-112).

### Zipline, swing and other rides: what the park publishes {#attractions}

The park's site lists Sky Bikes on a steel cable and a Cliff Swing, both paid separately, as well as slides, go-karts and an inflatable park.[^2] Its open pages give no age, weight or health rules for them, so treat them as you treat the bridge.

**Our tip.** Before you pay for any ride, ask at the desk for the age, weight and medical limits. Photograph the answer or the rules board.

## Hiking in Dashbashi Canyon: the trail down and the climb back {#trail}

The path is 1.8 km long, starts at the visitor centre, enters the canyon valley near the waterfall and takes about an hour there and back; georgia.travel and the Agency of Protected Areas agree on this.[^1][^5] The park recommends hiking or sports shoes.[^3]

<!-- схема: img/dashbashi-trail.webp | Infographic of the walk: 1.8 km, about one hour there and back, on the way to the trail: stairs, plan for the climb back as its time is not published, sports or hiking shoes recommended, water, a cap | The 1.8 km path takes about an hour there and back, so leave energy for the climb up -->

The climb back up is the part to plan for. No source we read gives a separate time for it or the height difference along the path. The range of 1,110–1,448 m is for the whole canyon, not for the walk. The Agency of Protected Areas adds that specially designated vehicles can carry visitors along the path if they wish;[^5] we found no details on where they run or what they cost.

The Government of Canada's travel advice says: "Trails are not always marked and weather conditions can change rapidly."[^7]

**Our tip.** Walk down in shoes with a ridged sole, with water and a cap. Climb back with stops if it is hot or the path is wet, and be out before the park closes.

See [Sprains, blisters and when to call 112](https://guide.100doc.ge/hiking-in-georgia/#injuries) and [Snakes, dogs and weather](https://guide.100doc.ge/hiking-in-georgia/#wildlife).

### Waterfalls and the Crying Wall {#waterfalls}

The path passes the waterfalls along the canyon walls.[^1] georgia.travel calls the curtain of small cascades on the rock the Crying Wall.[^1] It is also written Weeping Wall. The sources give individual falls no official names or heights.

For another canyon, in western Georgia, see [Martvili Canyon](https://guide.100doc.ge/martvili-canyon/).

## Dashbashi Canyon weather: heat, cold and ice {#weather}

georgia.travel gives an average of +16 degrees in summer and says the main waterfall freezes when the temperature drops to −6.[^1] We found no official climate norms for the canyon, so check the forecast on the day you go.

**Our tip.** Pack a warm jacket and a cap in the same bag, and take more water than you think you need. A warm day does not mean a dry path.

### Dashbashi Canyon in winter {#winter}

Nothing we found says the canyon closes for the season, but the main cascade freezes,[^1] and the park says the path may close when conditions are bad.[^3]

**Our tip.** Ice on the stairs and wet stone are slippery. Do not go onto the ice. Without non-slip soles, do not go down the stairs; if in doubt, stay at the top and ask the staff.

<!-- схема: img/dashbashi-frozen-waterfall.webp | Illustration: a frozen waterfall in a canyon on a frosty day, snow on the lava plateau and an icy staircase | In winter, when the temperature drops to −6, the main waterfall freezes -->

The road matters too: snow and heavy rain at higher altitudes can make travel difficult or impossible, the UK government's travel advice says.[^8] See [Night driving, mountains and winter](https://guide.100doc.ge/driving-in-georgia/#night-winter).

## Entrance fee and opening hours {#tickets}

We found no prices on the park's open pages. For a day visit, the park says no booking is needed.[^3] Hours and season can change, so check the site before you go.

## Medical help near the canyon: the emergency number and the nearest hospital {#medical-help}

In an emergency, dial 112 and ask for an ambulance, the UK government's travel advice says; it also warns that outside the capital medical facilities are limited.[^10] The Government of Canada adds that rural areas have shortages of medical supplies and personnel, and that first responders may not be able to reach them.[^7]

### Calling 112 from the canyon {#call-112}

It is the single emergency number: calls are taken 24/7 across the whole country, in Georgian, English, Azerbaijani, Armenian, Turkish and Russian.[^4] The emergency service says your phone must be within a mobile operator's coverage area;[^4] we have no data on coverage inside the canyon, so do not count on a signal.

**Our tip.** Say where you are first: "Dashbashi Canyon, near Dashbashi village, Tsalka Municipality". Then say whether you are on the path, at the bridge or at the visitor centre, how many of you there are and what happened. Send someone to meet the ambulance at the entrance. If your phone shows no network, move to where it does or ask someone at the visitor centre to call. More in [What to tell the operator](https://guide.100doc.ge/ambulance-georgia/#what-to-say).

### Where is the nearest hospital? {#nearest-hospital}

We found no official list of hospitals or first-aid posts for Tsalka Municipality, so we name none and give no distance. Clinics are listed for the capital, so for anything that is not an emergency expect a drive: the [catalogue of doctors and clinics in Tbilisi](https://100doc.ge/en/tbilisi/) had 574 clinics on 21 November 2026.

Since 1 January 2026, health and accident insurance of at least 30,000 GEL has been required for tourists entering Georgia, under [Resolution No. 602 of the Government of Georgia of 26 December 2025](https://matsne.gov.ge/ka/document/view/6728816).[^11] What the rule asks of you is explained in [Insurance to enter Georgia](https://guide.100doc.ge/#entry-insurance). The UK government also advises insurance that covers local treatment and unexpected medical evacuation.[^10]

In April 2026 the State Laboratory of Agriculture confirmed rabies in a fox in Tsalka Municipality, the National Food Agency said, as reported by the public broadcaster 1TV; a quarantine was declared at the time.[^12] After a bite or scratch, see [Rabies in Georgia](https://guide.100doc.ge/rabies-georgia/#first-aid).

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

### How do I get to Dashbashi Canyon from Tbilisi?

By car, taxi or a transfer: 92 km and about 1 h 40 min per the Agency of Protected Areas, about 90 minutes per the park.[^5][^3]

### Is there a glass bridge in Georgia?

Yes: the 240-metre Diamond Bridge over the canyon.[^1] Tbilisi's Bridge of Peace is a glass-clad pedestrian bridge, a different structure.[^9]

### Where is the Diamond Bridge?

In Tsalka Municipality, Kvemo Kartli, near Dashbashi village.[^3][^6]

### Is the glass bridge worth visiting?

That is for you to decide. The bridge is 240 m long and the walk down is 1.8 km;[^1] if you are afraid of heights, ask about the rules first.

### How much does it cost to enter?

We found no prices on the park's open pages; check its site.

### What are the opening hours?

Check the park's site; the path may close when conditions are bad.[^3]

### Can you go to the canyon in winter?

Nothing says it closes, but the main cascade freezes and ice is slippery.[^1][^3]

### What number do I call in an emergency?

112, 24/7, English spoken.[^4]
