> - The National Wine Agency of Georgia lists six wine-growing regions: Kakheti, Kartli, Imereti, Racha-Lechkhumi, the Black Sea coastal zone and Meskheti.[^1]
> - By our count, the agency's register of appellations has 33 entries, among them Khvanchkara.[^2]

## The wine regions of Georgia on one map {#map}

The National Wine Agency divides Georgia's wine country into six zones, most with sub-zones and each with an altitude band.[^1] The 2010 catalogue of the National Intellectual Property Center Sakpatenti, the patent office, maps ten administrative regions instead; this guide follows the agency's six zones.[^7]

Vineyards start at 2–4 m above sea level on the coast and reach 900–1,700 m in the highest zone.[^1]

## Kakheti wine region and the Alazani Valley {#kakheti}

Kakheti, Georgia's "premiere winegrowing region" as the Georgian National Tourism Administration describes it,[^20] has two sub-zones, Shida and Gare, and its best-quality vineyards lie at 400–700 m.[^1] Six of the eight protected micro-zones described below lie along the Alazani river, on one bank or the other.[^8][^9][^11][^13][^14][^15]

<!-- схема: img/georgian-wine-regions-alazani.webp | Illustration: rows of vines in a wide valley with baskets of dark and golden grapes at harvest time | The vintage season opens in this region in September -->

Among the region's popular grapes, the tourism board names local whites such as Kakhuri Mtsvane and Khikhvi.[^20] For Sighnaghi and its cellars, see [Kakheti wine: qvevri, Rkatsiteli and Saperavi](https://guide.100doc.ge/sighnaghi/#wine).

### Tsinandali, Mukuzani, Kindzmarauli: Kakheti's appellations {#kakheti-appellations}

Kindzmarauli is a naturally semi-sweet red from Saperavi grapes.[^10]

Mukuzani is a dry red made only from Saperavi; ageing in wooden barrels is optional, and if the wine is aged, it spends at least six months in them.[^9]

Tsinandali can be white or red, both dry. The white is at least 85 % Rkatsiteli with no more than 15 % Mtsvane Kakhuri, and the red is Saperavi only. Its micro-zone lies in Telavi municipality.[^8]

Four more in brief. Kvareli is a dry red.[^13] Teliani, also red and dry, is made from Cabernet Sauvignon rather than a local grape.[^15] Manavi is a dry white from Kakhuri Mtsvane only,[^12] and Napareuli can be white or red.[^11] The eighth name is covered under [Red, white, amber](#wine-types).

## Kartli and Imereti: the centre and the west {#kartli-imereti}

### Kartli wine {#kartli}

This central zone grows its vines at 450–700 m above sea level, in the basins of the Mtkvari, Liakhvi and Ksani rivers.[^16] It has three sub-zones: Kvemo (lower), Shida (inner) and Zemo (upper).[^1] Its protected name described here is Ateni (Atenuri): a white wine, dry, sparkling or slightly sparkling, from Chinuri, Goruli Mtsvane or Aligote, alone or blended.[^17]

### Imereti wine {#imereti}

This western zone has three sub-zones: Upper, Middle and Lower.[^1] The tourism board names Tsitska, Tsolikauri and Krakhuna as local whites with a natural sparkle;[^18] the agency's page names Sviri, made from the same three grapes, as this zone's protected name.[^1] For Kutaisi itself, see [Things to do in Kutaisi](https://guide.100doc.ge/kutaisi/#things-to-do).

## Racha-Lechkhumi: where Khvanchkara comes from {#racha}

Khvanchkara is a red, naturally semi-sweet wine made only from Aleksandrouli and Mujuretuli grapes near Ambrolauri.[^19] Its micro-zone lies in the lower part of the zone.[^1]

<!-- схема: img/georgian-wine-regions-racha.webp | Illustration: steep terraced vineyards above a narrow river valley with dark grapes in late autumn | The grapes for this appellation must contain at least 24 % sugar when picked -->

The tourism board calls it "legendary".[^18] The official register also has separate entries for the two sub-zones.[^2]

### Khvanchkara or Kindzmarauli? {#racha-vs}

Both are naturally semi-sweet reds; they differ in zone, grapes and limits. The first is made near Ambrolauri from Aleksandrouli and Mujuretuli, with 30–45 g/l of sugar and at least 11 % alcohol.[^19] The second is a Kakheti red with 18–45 g/l of sugar and at least 10.5 % alcohol.[^10]

## The Black Sea coast and Meskheti {#west}

These are the last two of the agency's six zones.[^1] The coastal zone joins four historical regions, among them Samegrelo and Abkhazeti, with vineyards from 2–4 m up to 500 m above sea level.[^1] The other zone lies much higher, at 900–1,700 m.[^1]

The agency's page names local grapes of the coast, such as Chkhaveri, Jani and Ojaleshi, and adds that some scientists think Saperavi and Dzvelshavi could have origins in the higher zone.[^1] The vintage season ends on the coast in December, when Chkhaveri is picked.[^4]

## Protected appellations: names on the label {#appellations}

An appellation of origin, or PDO, is the name of a micro-zone reserved for wine made from set grapes in a set style. PDO stands for protected designation of origin. By our count, the agency's register lists 33 entries, though four show no registration number, and one of those is still a pending application.[^2] One of the latest entries, Sazanos Otskhanuri, dates from 30 September 2025.[^2] In 2010, Sakpatenti counted 18 registered names,[^7] so the list has grown since.

Reading a label is simple: the name tells you the zone, the grapes and the wine type at once, as the [table of zones](#map) shows. Grape spirit has its own page: [What is chacha? Georgia's grape spirit](https://guide.100doc.ge/chacha-georgia/#what-is-chacha).

## Georgian grape varieties: Saperavi, Rkatsiteli and more {#grapes}

Saperavi is the main red grape and Rkatsiteli the main white one by harvest volume. In 2024 the agency reported about 320,000 tonnes of grapes processed, which it called a record for independent Georgia; about 136,000 tonnes were the main white and 130,000 tonnes the main red.[^21]

Official pages give two counts of local varieties: over 500 described, and 525 unique local ones.[^22] There is no single official figure.

Other local whites named on official pages include Khikhvi and Kisi,[^20][^18] Chinuri from the central zone,[^17] and Tsitska, Tsolikauri and Krakhuna in the west.[^18]

### Red, white, amber: dry or semi-sweet? {#wine-types}

Most wines described in this guide are dry. The naturally semi-sweet ones are Kindzmarauli and Khvanchkara; Akhasheni is semi-sweet.[^10][^19][^14] For skin-contact wine, see [Qvevri and amber wine](#qvevri).

## Qvevri and amber wine: the UNESCO method {#qvevri}

A qvevri is an egg-shaped earthenware vessel used for making, ageing and storing wine.[^3] Grapes, juice, skins, stalks and pips go into the vessel, which is sealed and buried in the ground; the wine can ferment for five to six months.[^3] The vessel is closed with a clay lid.[^18]

<!-- схема: img/georgian-wine-regions-qvevri.webp | Illustration: cross-section of an egg-shaped clay qvevri buried in the ground up to its neck and sealed with a clay lid | Clay-vessel wine-making has been on the intangible cultural heritage list since 2013 -->

Families pass the method down, it is practised across the country, especially in villages, and the wine cellar is treated as the holiest place in the family home.[^3]

Amber (orange) wine is made by long maceration on the skins. It has been added to the list of special wines of the International Organisation of Vine and Wine (OIV).[^23] For toasts and food, see [The supra, wine and drinks](https://guide.100doc.ge/georgian-food/#supra-drinks).

### How old is Georgian wine? {#history}

Archaeologists dated traces of grape wine in clay jars to about 6000–5800 BC at two sites roughly 50 km south of Tbilisi, Shulaveris Gora and Gadachrili Gora.[^24] The 2017 study calls this the earliest biomolecular evidence of grape wine in the Near East. Its authors say the jars probably held wine and do not give priority to any one region.[^24]

The agency's own promotional message is "8 thousand continuous vintages".[^22]

## Rtveli: when to visit the wine regions {#rtveli}

The vintage season runs from September to December: it opens in Kakheti and ends with Chkhaveri on the Black Sea coast, and travellers can take part.[^4] We found no official calendar with dates for each year and zone, so check locally before you plan around a specific day.

For autumn travel in general, see [Spring and autumn: Georgia in May and October](https://guide.100doc.ge/best-time-to-visit-georgia/#spring-autumn).

### Heat, water and tasting in the sun {#heat}

Alcohol and long hours in the sun make dehydration more likely, and drinking fluids regularly lowers the risk.[^25] On a harvest day or a daytime cellar visit, drink water regularly.

In hot weather, avoid drinking too much alcohol, keep out of the sun from 11:00 to 15:00 and get urgent help for signs of heatstroke.[^26]

<!-- схема: img/georgian-wine-regions-rtveli-heat.webp | Infographic card "Rtveli: September to December": a timeline from September, Rkatsiteli harvest starts in Kakheti, to December, Chkhaveri harvest ends the season in Guria and Adjara; cards below: drink water regularly (NHS); alcohol and heat raise the risk of dehydration (NHS); keep out of the sun 11:00–15:00 (NHS); water quality varies, bottled water is widely sold (UK government); emergency: 112 | The grape harvest runs September to December; keep out of the sun from 11:00 to 15:00 -->

Water quality varies, bottled water is widely sold, and outside Tbilisi medical facilities are limited.[^27] For the weather and signs of overheating, see [Telavi in summer: weather, heat and how to stay well](https://guide.100doc.ge/telavi/#summer-heat). If you need a doctor in the region, the [catalogue of clinics in Telavi on 100doc.ge](https://100doc.ge/en/telavi/) lists local clinics; if a life is at risk, call 112.[^27]

**Our tip.** Carry a bottle of water for each person, taste after a meal and in the shade, and spit or skip glasses. With children, bring hats and plan shade breaks.

## Getting there and driving after a tasting {#driving}

The road trip takes under two hours.[^20] A day of wine needs a driver who does not drink.

### How do I get from Tbilisi to Kakheti? {#from-tbilisi}

The tourism board gives about 1 hour 50 minutes by road from the capital, without naming a town in the region.[^20] If you plan to drink, go with a hired driver or by public transport.

For routes and travel times, see [Tbilisi to Telavi: how to get there and how long it takes](https://guide.100doc.ge/telavi/#getting-there); for minibuses, see [Didube: where many minibuses leave from](https://guide.100doc.ge/marshrutka-georgia/#where).

### Wine tasting in Georgia and the drink-drive limit {#tasting-driving}

Don't drink if you drive in Georgia: the law treats more than 0.3 ‰ of alcohol in a driver's blood as drunk driving.[^5] Written as a percentage, that is 0.03 %. Under Article 116 of the Code of Administrative Offences, over 0.3 and up to 0.7 ‰ means a six-month suspension of the right to drive, and over 0.7 ‰ a one-year suspension.[^5]

The Law on Road Traffic bans driving while drunk. It obliges a driver to take a sobriety test when an authorised police officer has sufficient grounds to suspect drunkenness.[^28]

**What governments advise.** According to the Government of Canada, "Georgia has a zero-tolerance policy for drinking and driving." It lists heavy fines, detention and a driving ban "for several months or years", reports that fatal accidents are frequent and advises travellers to avoid travelling after dark.[^6] The UK government says Georgia's limit is lower than the UK's and advises avoiding alcohol if you drive.[^29]

The law sets a threshold; the Canadian page describes no tolerance at all. Either way, don't drink if you drive.

To avoid a hangover, see [Wine tasting without the hangover: NHS advice](https://guide.100doc.ge/sighnaghi/#tasting); for the road rules on alcohol, see [Drink driving](https://guide.100doc.ge/driving-in-georgia/#alcohol).

**Our tip.** Decide who stays sober before the first cellar, or hire a driver for the day.

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

### Are there five or six wine regions in Georgia?

Six, by the official count: from the east of the country to the Black Sea coast.[^1]

### What is the most famous Georgian wine?

The official register does not rank the names. Among the names in it are Khvanchkara, a red, and Tsinandali, a dry white or red.[^2][^19][^8]

### Is Georgian wine the oldest in the world?

That is not proven. Archaeologists found traces of grape wine from about 6000–5800 BC at two Neolithic sites in Georgia, the earliest such evidence in the Near East.[^24] The authors do not claim it is the oldest anywhere.[^24]

### When is the best time to visit the wine regions?

The grape harvest runs from September to December.[^4] In warm weather, drink water regularly.[^25]

### How do I travel from Tbilisi to Kakheti?

By road, in about 1 hour 50 minutes, according to the tourism board.[^20] If you plan to drink, take a driver or public transport.

### Can I drive after a wine tasting in Georgia?

No. More than 0.3 ‰ counts as drunk driving, with a 6- or 12-month suspension; avoiding the test also costs a year, and repeat offences can mean detention.[^5] The Government of Canada calls Georgia's policy "zero-tolerance".[^6]
