Ananuri Fortress and Zhinvali Reservoir: the road from Tbilisi, the water and medical help

Ananuri is a village and fortress in Georgia's Mtskheta-Mtianeti region, on the Aragvi River at the shore of Zhinvali Reservoir. The Georgian Military Road runs through it, and it is about 60 km from Tbilisi, a drive of about 1 h 25 min, according to the Georgian National Tourism Administration. This guide covers how to get there and back without a tour, the roadside and the road in winter, the water below the walls, and who to call if something goes wrong.

Where is Ananuri and how far is it from Tbilisi?

The village belongs to Dusheti Municipality and sits in the mountains north of the capital: about 60 km away and 1 h 25 min by road, by the tourism administration's figures. Other sources give other distances, so we use this one.

The administration places it on the right bank of the river, with the main road north passing straight through. The lake below the fortress is Zhinvali Reservoir, an artificial lake held back by a dam. Its water and whether you can go in are covered further down.

Ananuri Fortress: what you see inside the walls

Often called Ananuri castle, it is a fortified ensemble rather than one building. The tourism administration lists upper and lower fortresses, the walls, the towers, three churches, a bell tower, an octagonal cell and a water reservoir.

The same page calls it a feudal stronghold that controlled the roads around it. The administration also notes the view of the reservoir from the fortress.

The Tentative List entry, submitted by Georgia, describes a walled ensemble with turrets, several churches and a tower with a Svanetian-style roof.

How old is Ananuri Fortress?

Georgia's tourism administration dates the buildings to between the 14th and 18th centuries. Its page on the village says the 16th to 18th, and the Tentative List entry submitted by Georgia gives the 17th century, so treat any single date as approximate.

The fortress's history includes a royal episode: the administration writes that King Erekle II named it among the seven strongest fortresses in the country and took refuge there after the Battle of Krtsanisi.

We give no year for individual buildings and no names of builders, because the sources do not agree even on the century.

Is it a UNESCO World Heritage Site?

No. According to its entry on the UNESCO World Heritage Centre site, Georgia's Ministry of Culture, Monuments Protection and Sport submitted it to the Tentative List on 24 October 2007; it is not inscribed as a World Heritage Site.

The entry, written by the Georgian side, notes that "the visual perception of the ensemble has been changed" by the building of the reservoir, and that "the wall painting needs conservation".

Ananuri entrance fee and opening hours

There is no official ticket price or visiting times that we could check, and unofficial websites contradict each other. Check on the spot.

Our tip. Dress modestly when you go inside, as you would for any Orthodox church in Georgia. See Visiting churches for what that means in practice.

Tbilisi to Ananuri: by car, taxi or marshrutka, and the way back

Drive or take a taxi north along the main road. The road runs through the village, so minibuses heading towards Kazbegi pass along it, but whether they stop here is not officially confirmed, and no timetable or fare for this stop is published that we know of.

By car or taxi

This is the simplest option, because the return is in your hands. The road leaves the capital northwards past Mtskheta and then follows the river valley into the mountains.

The UK government's travel advice warns that "roads outside city centres are often badly lit and poor quality". What that means behind the wheel is covered in Georgia roads: what to expect. With a taxi, agree in advance whether the driver waits while you look round.

Our tip. Drive there and back in daylight, and set off early enough that the return does not fall after dark.

By marshrutka: getting there and getting back

A shared van heading north uses the road through the village, but getting off there is not officially confirmed, and getting back is harder still: nothing official tells you where or when a van towards the capital will pick you up.

Many shared vans in the capital leave from around Didube metro station; see Didube: where many minibuses leave from. No official source names the departure point for this route, and we do not repeat the fares quoted on unofficial websites.

The Government of Canada's travel advice is blunt: "Mini-buses called 'marshrutka' are poorly maintained and often lack security features like seatbelts. Avoid using them."

Our tip. Arrange the return before you leave the capital: a taxi that waits for you, or a driver for the whole day. Do not count on a free seat in a passing van. If bends make you feel sick, read Motion sickness on mountain roads first.

Ananuri as a day trip, or a stop on the way to Kazbegi

Both work: a short stop on the drive north, or a day trip on its own. The tourism administration gives 1 h 25 min from the capital; the return should take about as long.

Our tip. Winter days are short: check when it gets dark before you set off.

If you are going further into the mountains, see Tbilisi to Kazbegi: the Georgian Military Road. For the ski resort on the way, see How high is Gudauri?

Is the road to Ananuri safe? Stopping, the roadside and winter

The answer depends on the day, the light and the driver. The UK government's travel advice for Georgia warns that "heavy rain, flooding and snow at higher altitudes can affect roads and bridges making travel difficult or impossible" and that "landslides are common".

For taxis and vans in general, see Is Georgia safe?

Stopping by the road and waiting for a minibus

What follows is advice only: we know of no official rules or figures for this roadside.

Our tip.

  • Pull over only where the whole car is off the carriageway.
  • Walk and take photos away from the traffic lane, never on the carriageway itself.
  • Hold children by the hand near the road.
  • If it is getting dark, do not stand at the roadside: call a taxi instead.

Ananuri in winter: weather and the road further north

Check the weather forecast on the day you travel: there is no official climate data for the village that we could use. Higher up, the UK government warns, snow can make travel difficult or impossible.

The Roads Department of Georgia publishes current limits on its restrictions page. When we checked on 21 November 2026, its latest entries concerned the Gudauri (Posta)–Kobi section: traffic for vehicles with trailers was restricted on 19 November and restored on 20 November. There were no entries for this village at the time of checking; this is a snapshot, not a forecast. The department's hotline is 032 2 31 30 76.

In snow and storms, closures happen further north, on the pass: see The Jvari Pass and road closures. Driving yourself in winter? Read Night driving, mountains and winter first.

Summer has its own risk. The UK government's travel advice says: "Lightning and wildfires are a risk in summer, particularly in the mountains."

Zhinvali Reservoir: the lake below Ananuri and the capital's drinking water

It is an artificial lake on the Aragvi River (also called Zhinvali Lake and spelled Jinvali), held back by the Zhinvali Dam. The dam was built in 1985, is 102 m high and holds around 500 million m³; its hydropower plant is rated at 130 MW.

The World Bank Group's MIGA describes the reservoir and dam as the capital's "primary storage source of potable water". Other sources give a different year for the dam; we use MIGA's. We give no area, depth or share of the city's supply. The tourism administration mentions picnic tables and boat rental at the shore.

The water is protected by local rules. Dusheti Municipal Council has approved rules on sanitary protection zones for water-supply sources in the river valley (Decision No. 1 of 26 January 2024). Its amendment, Decision No. 10 of 25 April 2025, names the reservoir as a water-supply source and deals with wastewater, extraction of inert materials, information signs and waste collection.

Can you swim in Zhinvali Reservoir?

We found no rule on swimming in the official documents we could read. The 2025 amendment does not mention it, and we could not find the base rules of January 2024 to check them. There are no fishing rules specific to this reservoir that we know of either.

What is certain is that this is the capital's main water store, according to MIGA, and that the rules provide for information signs in the protection zone.

Our tip.

  • This water ends up in the capital's taps: take your rubbish with you and do not wash dishes at the water's edge.
  • If you go into the water, never go alone and never after drinking alcohol.
  • Keep children within arm's reach, and follow any signs on the shore.
  • If someone gets into trouble, call the emergency number at once.

Medical help near Ananuri: 112 and the nearest clinics

In an emergency, dial 112 and ask for an ambulance; the UK government's travel advice puts it in exactly those words. The same advice warns that outside the capital medical facilities are limited.

Calling 112 on the road

One number covers every emergency, and you can speak English. Operators take calls in Georgian, English, Azerbaijani, Armenian, Turkish and Russian, 24/7 across the whole country, but the phone must be within a mobile operator's coverage area, the emergency service says. The tourism administration says the call is free from any phone; the Ministry of Health's hotline, 1505, answers 24 hours a day.

Our tip. Say where you are first: "Ananuri village, Dusheti Municipality, on the Georgian Military Road". Then give your direction, towards the capital or north into the mountains, and a landmark: the fortress or the reservoir. If your phone shows no network, move to where it does. More in What to tell the operator.

Where is the nearest hospital?

We found no official list of hospitals for the municipality and no official information on non-emergency care near the village, so we name none. The UK government's list of medical facilities in Georgia, updated 16 October 2026, has none in this region: all are in the capital and Batumi, and the list says it is not complete.

The clinics we can point to are in the capital: the catalogue of doctors and clinics in Tbilisi listed 574 clinics when we checked on 21 November 2026.

Since 1 January 2026, tourists entering Georgia must have health and accident cover of at least 30,000 GEL, with some exceptions such as diplomatic passport holders, under Resolution No. 602 of the Government of Georgia of 26 December 2025. What the rule asks of you is explained in Insurance to enter Georgia. The UK government also advises insurance that covers local treatment and medical evacuation.

Frequently asked questions

Where is Ananuri located?

In the mountains north of the capital, at the shore of the reservoir, with the main road north running through the village.

How far is Ananuri from Tbilisi?

About 60 km, or 1 h 25 min by road, according to the tourism administration. Other sources give other distances.

How old is Ananuri Fortress?

The tourism administration dates the buildings to the 14th–18th centuries; other sources disagree.

How do I get to Ananuri and back to Tbilisi?

By car or taxi is simplest. Northbound shared vans use the road, but there is no official timetable or stop for them at the village, so arrange the return before you set off.

Is there an entrance fee at Ananuri Fortress?

We found no official fee or opening hours, so check on the spot.

Is it possible to swim in Zhinvali Reservoir?

No rule either way appears in the official documents we could read. The reservoir is the capital's main store of drinking water. If you go in, never swim alone, and in an emergency dial 112.

Is it safe to drive from Tbilisi to Kazbegi?

It is a mountain road, and the answer depends on the conditions and the light. The UK government's travel advice warns about poorly lit roads, snow higher up and landslides. In winter, check the Roads Department's restrictions page on the day.
