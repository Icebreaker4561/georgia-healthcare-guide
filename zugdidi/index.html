Zugdidi guide: Dadiani Palace, the road to Mestia, the Abkhazia line and medical help

Zugdidi is the administrative capital of the Samegrelo Region in western Georgia, 4:50 by road from Tbilisi according to Georgia's national tourism site. This guide explains Dadiani Palace and its garden, the railway and the road, the drive up to Mestia, the Abkhazia line and medical help. Facts about the town come from Georgia's national tourism site, the railway operator and, where marked, Wikipedia; safety advice comes from the UK government and the Government of Canada; the Law on Occupied Territories comes from Georgia's official legal portal. Sources are at the end. Checked on 22 November 2026.

Where is Zugdidi, Georgia?

In the west of the country. Georgia's national tourism site calls it "the administrative capital of the Samegrelo Region". According to Wikipedia, the town lies 318 km west of Tbilisi and 30 km from the Black Sea coast.

The wider region. The GNTA regional page names the town as one of the two centres of Samegrelo-Zemo Svaneti.

Altitude. Wikipedia puts the town at 100–110 metres above sea level.

Population. About 40,100 people live in the town, according to Wikipedia, as of 1 January 2024. We found no official figure for the wider municipality.

Travel times. By road, it is 4:50 from Tbilisi. More below.

Dadiani Palace in Zugdidi: the museum and the garden

It is a Neo-Gothic palace in the centre of the town, and today it is a museum. The national tourism site describes the building as "Neo-Gothic" and says it "stands in the centre of the city".

Dadiani Palace museum: what is inside

The personal belongings of the princely family. "Today it acts as a Palace-Museum where you can see the Dadianis' personal items," the national tourism site says.

The building. "Built in the 17th century, the palace underwent a comprehensive reconstruction under the guidance of the English architect Edwin Race in the 19th century," according to the same site. Wikipedia, a secondary source, dates the building to the 19th century. We follow the national tourism site.

The region's history. The same page calls it one of the first European-style palaces built in the historical territory of Odishi, "today known as Zugdidi".

The collection. The museum "contains more than 44,475 antique objects", the GNTA page on the residence and the garden reports. Among them, the page adds, are "one of Napoleon Bonaparte's private belongings and also one of his death masks".

A state museum. The regional page of the same administration puts it plainly: "The palace is now a state museum".

Zugdidi Botanical Garden

The garden lies right beside the palace and was set up in 1840, by the GNTA account. The national tourism site describes "the botanical garden, situated right next to the palace, which was created in imitation of the Bois De Boulogne Park in Paris".

How big it is. The figures differ. Unique plants grow "across all 67 hectares of the gardens", according to the national tourism site; Wikipedia gives 26 hectares.

Is there an official timetable for opening hours and tickets?

No. We found no opening hours or ticket prices on an official site. The national tourism site gives no hours, prices or phone number for the museum, and we did not find a museum website of its own.

Our tip. Check the hours and the ticket price with the museum itself on the day of your visit.

Things to do in Zugdidi and nearby

The palace and its garden in the centre; nearby, a dam to the north and the Black Sea coast, according to the national tourism site.

The Enguri dam

North of the town stands a dam that has been declared a cultural monument. "To the north of the city, you will find a unique dam on the Enguri River," the national tourism site says.

Why it matters. "The Enguri Hydroelectric Power Plant has been declared a cultural monument," the site adds, "as it is the world's third-highest concrete arch dam".

Tours. "Tours of the dam are available, as well as boat tours out onto the lake," according to the same page. It gives no prices or timetable.

Anaklia and the Black Sea

The coast is close. "A thirty-minute drive from the city is Anaklia, a resort town on the Black Sea," the national tourism site says. For the beach and swimming, read our guide to the resort.

Zugdidi weather and climate

Humid subtropical. Wikipedia, a secondary source, classes the climate as Cfa under the Köppen system.

The averages. Over the year, Wikipedia gives an average high of 20.6 °C and an average low of 10.4 °C, for 1991–2020. We found no official climate norms for the town.

Hot months. For heat and storms across the country, see Georgia in summer: heat, UV and storms.

Our tip. If you are heading on into the mountains, check the forecast for the mountains, not for the lowland town you leave from.

How to get to Zugdidi

By rail or by road. Fast service 870/869 runs daily from Tbilisi, and by road the national tourism site gives 4:50 from Tbilisi, 1:40 from Kutaisi and 2:00 from Batumi.

Tbilisi to Zugdidi train

One fast service runs every day. The Georgian Railway general schedule lists "870/869 Tbilisi-Zugdidi" under "Fast Trains", with the note "Daily".

Journey time. The general timetable does not mark which times belong to which direction, so we give no journey time.

Routes and tickets. See Routes and How to buy tickets in our guide to Georgian Railway.

By road from Tbilisi, Kutaisi and Batumi

The national tourism site gives times, not kilometres: 4:50 from Tbilisi, 1:40 from Kutaisi and 2:00 from Batumi.

Kutaisi airport. The GNTA airports page places the airport "in the west of Kutaisi, 14 kilometers away from the city". We found no official time for the airport-to-town leg. See Arriving at Kutaisi airport.

From Batumi. See the travel section of our Batumi guide.

Bus and marshrutka. We found no official timetable or fares for buses or minibuses on these routes. See What official advice says about marshrutkas.

Zugdidi to Mestia: the gateway to Svaneti

About three hours by road. The GNTA page on the mountain town describes a "3-hour-long drive" from Zugdidi that "sees the environment change from subtropical to subalpine".

The road. Beyond the dam, "a road goes between the cliffs and the rushing Enguri River, leading you to Mestia and Ushguli", the site's page on this town reads. The official pages give no distance in kilometres, so we give none.

Before you go. Read Getting there and The Svan towers in our mountain guide, and Night driving, mountains and winter.

Our tip. Leave in the morning to have daylight for the road. If you hire a driver, agree on the trip before you set off.

Zugdidi and the Abkhazia line: what governments advise

Both governments quoted here advise against travel to the occupied region. Neither says anything about the town itself.

The UK government. Its travel advice says: "For security reasons, FCDO advises against all travel to the Russian occupied regions", and it names South Ossetia and Abkhazia. The UK government (FCDO) "also advises against all but essential travel to areas immediately next to the administrative boundary lines".

The Government of Canada. Its travel advice says to avoid all travel to the occupied regions of Abkhazia and South Ossetia and to areas "within 5 km of the administrative boundary line", "excluding Highway E60". The reasons it gives: "crime, military activity, the risk of detention and the presence of landmines". It also warns that the checkpoints "are subject to unexpected closures", and that landmines "may pose a risk where military operations have occurred".

What the advice does not say. Neither government names Zugdidi or says whether the town lies inside these zones. We found no official source on whether the crossing points with Abkhazia work today.

Can you go to Abkhazia from Zugdidi?

Under Georgian law, foreigners may enter the occupied region only from the direction of Zugdidi municipality; every other direction is prohibited. Both governments above advise against going at all.

The law. We quote Article 4 of the Law of Georgia on Occupied Territories (English translation on Matsne; the latest consolidated Georgian text, of 26 June 2025, says the same). It lets foreign citizens in "only through the below route". For this region, the route is "from Zugdidi municipality direction".

Other directions. Entry from any other direction "shall be prohibited and be punishable under the Criminal Code of Georgia", says the same article. "In a particular case", it adds, a person may be granted "special permission to enter the occupied territories from the prohibited directions".

Unofficial crossings. The Government of Canada warns that entering Georgia from these regions at an unofficial crossing can bring "heavy fines and jail sentences".

No help on the spot. The UK government says it "cannot provide face-to-face support to British nationals" in the occupied regions.

For the rest of the country, see Where not to go: Abkhazia and South Ossetia. Near the coast, see The Abkhazia line near Anaklia.

Our tip. Do not try to cross the administrative boundary line. Before a trip towards the line, read the current advice of your government.

Hospitals and medical help in Zugdidi

In an emergency, dial 112. On the national tourism site's list, the town has Zugdidi Referral Hospital.

Calling 112 in Zugdidi

One number works around the clock, and its call-takers can handle calls in English. Georgia's emergency service takes calls 24/7 throughout the country; its "call-takers can handle calls in Georgian, English, Azerbaijani, Armenian, Turkish and Russian".

The UK government's health advice puts it simply: "Dial 112 and ask for an ambulance". Step by step: How to call an ambulance.

Our tip. Say the name of the town first, then the street or a landmark, such as the palace.

Where is the hospital in Zugdidi?

On K. Gamsakhurdia Street. The GNTA medical information page lists the Referral Hospital at K. Gamsakhurdia St. №206.

What we could not confirm. We found no official word on whether it works around the clock or how it receives foreign patients. In an emergency, call the ambulance first.

Outside the capital. The UK government warns: "Outside Tbilisi, medical facilities are limited". The Government of Canada adds: "In rural areas, there are shortages of medical supplies and personnel".

Clinics. The catalogue of clinics in Zugdidi on 100doc.ge listed 39 clinics on 22 November 2026.

Dogs and rabies

Keep away from dogs. The UK government says rabies "treatment is available in Tbilisi and major towns".

The Government of Canada warns: "Rabies is present in this destination and is carried by dogs and some wildlife, including bats". If an animal bites or scratches you, read What to do after a dog bite.

Your cover. Visitors need health insurance of no less than 30,000 GEL (Georgian lari), says the UK government in its entry advice. More in Insurance to Enter Georgia (2026 Rule).

Our tip.

  • Bring enough of your regular medicines for the whole trip.
  • Before the mountains, save the emergency number and the K. Gamsakhurdia Street address in your phone.
  • In the heat, drink water and keep to the shade. See Tap water and bottled water.

Frequently asked questions

Where is Zugdidi?

In western Georgia. It is the administrative capital of the Samegrelo Region.

What is Zugdidi known for?

For the Neo-Gothic palace of the Dadiani family, now a Palace-Museum, and for the botanical garden beside it.

How do I get from Tbilisi to Zugdidi?

By the daily fast train 870/869, or by road in 4:50. We found no official bus timetable.

How do I get from Zugdidi to Mestia?

By road, in about three hours. We found no official marshrutka timetable or fares.

How do I travel between Kutaisi and Zugdidi?

By road it takes 1:40. Kutaisi airport lies 14 km from Kutaisi.

What is the weather like in Zugdidi?

Humid subtropical (Cfa in the Köppen classification); the averages are in the weather section above.

Can I visit Abkhazia from Zugdidi?

The UK government and the Government of Canada advise against it. Under Georgian law, foreigners may enter only from this municipality's direction; any other route is punishable under the Criminal Code of Georgia.

Where is the hospital in Zugdidi?

Zugdidi Referral Hospital is at K. Gamsakhurdia St. №206.

What number do I call in an emergency?

Dial 112. It takes calls 24/7 across Georgia, and its call-takers can handle calls in English.
